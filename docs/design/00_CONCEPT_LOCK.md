# Signal 45 — Concept Lock

**Stage:** Prompt 1 foundation

**Status:** Design direction locked; fun, balance, performance, and production feasibility remain unproven until prototyped.

**Date:** 2026-07-18

## Requirement status

- **Confirmed owner requirements:** title, mobile-first landscape format, 45-day campaign, side-view 2.5D cutaway rendered with a true 3D world, base-building priority, five stock resources, four resident conditions, offline pause, ethical monetization boundary, and no combat power fantasy.
- **Design decisions made here:** the exact pitches, five-part identity, day rhythm, seven room families for the slice, named starting cast, route identities, failure process, and vertical-slice proof.
- **Assumptions:** one authored coastal interchange is the launch map; one in-game day normally fits one deliberate mobile session; the full game targets 10–14 residents; and the visual target uses modular, reusable 3D production rather than bespoke realism everywhere.
- **Prototype validation required:** session timing, work capacity, utility readability, route balance, camera comfort, crowd/performance targets, expedition presentation, and whether the core loop is enjoyable.

## Final pitch

### One sentence

In **Signal 45**, coordinate a small community through 45 days of cinder storms by reclaiming a dead railway interchange into a living underground home, spending scarce radio attention on uncertain warnings, and deciding whether to trust an evacuation train that may never come.

### Fifteen-second spoken pitch

Build a home inside a ruined underground station, keep a handful of named survivors alive through heat and cinder storms, and choose which radio signals to trust. Everything you construct, promise, and discover shapes what happens when Day 45 arrives.

### Store description (approximately 100 words)

Extreme heat and airborne cinder storms have driven the last survivors beneath a shattered coastal city. As coordinator of an abandoned railway interchange, reclaim flooded platforms, restore utilities, construct rooms, and turn cold concrete into a living home. Assign named residents according to their skills and limits, prepare for dangerous weather, and venture outside during brief nighttime windows. Your damaged Listening Post can follow only a fraction of the broadcasts it receives—including a disputed promise that an evacuation train will arrive on Day 45. What you build, whom you shelter, which signals you trust, and which promises you keep will decide the community’s future.

## Locked identity

| Element | Decision |
|---|---|
| Title | **Signal 45**. Never abbreviated or renamed in player-facing use. |
| Genre | Mobile-first survival management, railway shelter construction, community simulation, narrative strategy, and supporting scavenging expeditions. |
| Platforms and format | iOS and Android first, landscape orientation; PC is a later possibility only. Normal shelter sessions target approximately 5–12 minutes with frequent safe stops. |
| Business model | Premium purchase, or a free seven-day introductory chapter followed by one permanent full-game unlock. No paid survival relief, energy, revivals, residents, loot boxes, mandatory ads, or speedups. |
| Setting | A damaged underground railway interchange below a climate-struck coastal city where daytime heat and recurring airborne cinder storms make surface travel dangerous. |
| Player role | The station’s elected or accepted **coordinator**: a scheduler, builder, listener, and accountable decision-maker whose authority remains conditional. |
| Player fantasy | Turn a dead station into a visibly different underground town whose people survive because the player understood the place, prepared well, and respected human limits. |
| Emotional promise | Pressure without nihilism: dread gives way to competence, attachment, recovery, and earned warmth, while hard decisions leave specific human traces. |
| Signature hook | **Scarce attention links intelligence to construction.** The Listening Post cannot follow every signal, so information chosen today changes tomorrow’s rooms, routes, expeditions, visitors, and ending preparations. |
| Campaign objective | Reach Day 45 with a functioning community and enough verified knowledge, infrastructure, relationships, and contingency capacity to choose a credible future. |

## Success states and failure process

Success is plural, but never arbitrary. A campaign succeeds when the community reaches a viable Day 45 resolution supported by what was actually built and learned. Possible launch-game resolution families are:

1. **Evacuate:** verify and safely meet a real evacuation opportunity with the people and supplies needed to travel.
2. **Remain:** reject or lose the train but establish a defensible, supplied, socially viable settlement.
3. **Relay:** connect the station to a wider mutual-aid network and become an information or transit node rather than merely escaping.
4. **Survive at a cost:** preserve life but accept losses, fractured relationships, or a precarious future reflected in the ending.

Failure is a readable process, not a surprise roll. Shortages and damage first create forecasts and warnings, then degraded performance, incidents, injury or illness, relationship consequences, and finally an unrecoverable station state if the player repeatedly declines available interventions. A campaign-ending failure requires a causal recap and returns to a recent safe checkpoint. Individual death is possible in the full game only after legible danger, response time, and sustained risk; the seven-day slice tests injury, illness, treatment, and recovery instead of random death.

## Core differentiators

1. **Railway-native reclamation:** track beds, platforms, drainage, signal rooms, lifts, stairs, utility trunks, and sealed wings determine useful constraints rather than serving as decoration.
2. **A shelter that visibly becomes home:** construction changes traversal, utility routes, safety, activity, lighting, possessions, and social use—not only production numbers.
3. **Information as a limited operational resource:** signals compete for listening commitment and feed the shelter simulation rather than opening a disconnected text mode.
4. **Specific people with conditional cooperation:** named residents remember promises, negotiate dangerous work, recover in personal ways, and may refuse for understandable reasons.
5. **Preparedness over combat:** layout, warnings, maintenance, protection, negotiation, and retreat prevent most harm; force is brief, costly, and never a progression fantasy.
6. **One simulation for active and delegated nightruns:** the same spatial route graph, risks, tools, and persistent location state support both styles.
7. **A campaign ending built over 45 days:** Day 45 evaluates infrastructure, evidence, promises, population, and contingency plans rather than presenting a detached final morality button.

## Concept audit

### Contradictions resolved

| Tension | Resolution |
|---|---|
| Base management is primary, but active expeditions could become a second game. | Nightruns reuse a compact node graph, occur only in chosen night windows, and return information or materials that answer a declared shelter need. One graph supports active and delegated play. Slice content is one location, not an expedition campaign. |
| The view must read as 2D while the world is truly 3D. | Simulation is locked to authored side-on lanes and room volumes. 3D exists for depth, lighting, animation, damage, and Room Focus—not free camera orbit or fully navigable 3D architecture. |
| The shelter must physically grow but cannot be blank-grid excavation. | The outer station is authored; reclamation exposes adaptable bays. The player chooses bay purpose, equipment, modules, access restoration, utility branches, and repurposing. |
| `Charge` is a stock while `Power` is a utility. | Power is instantaneous connected capacity/demand and network condition. Charge is stored reserve measured in abstract cell-hours, spent only when generation is insufficient or deliberately buffered. |
| Serious realism could make mobile information unreadable. | Shelter Overview prioritizes silhouettes and state symbols; Room Focus carries material detail. One utility overlay at a time and contextual action panels prevent dashboard accumulation. |
| A variable Signal 45 truth could make preparation feel pointless. | Campaign truth may vary only among authored scenario packages with consistent evidence. Preparation categories remain useful across variants, and the ending explains which evidence was accurate and why. |
| The coordinator has authority, but residents are autonomous. | Ordinary safe work can be assigned; dangerous, boundary-crossing, or promise-breaking orders trigger negotiation or refusal with a stated reason. Refusal is systemic and predictable, not random. |
| A 45-day campaign conflicts with 5–12 minute sessions. | Each day is structured around safe phase boundaries and normally fits one session; optional active nightruns extend chosen sessions. Exact timing is reserved for Prompt 2. |

### Missing pieces deliberately handed forward

- Exact day and phase duration, work units, production and consumption rates, and construction costs.
- Deterministic East/West feasibility and recovery math.
- Exact Highball speed benefit and fatigue/stress cost.
- Save schema, simulation tick rate, device performance budgets, and content-authoring tools.
- Full resident behavior architecture, complete crafting balance, full expedition implementation, final graphics, voice scope, and store packaging.
- Exact number and composition of Signal 45 truth variants; the slice needs one authored truth chain only.

### Excessive scope removed or bounded

- Seven slice room families replace dozens of bespoke room systems.
- Four starting residents and one admission candidate replace a broad recruit roster.
- One station, two first-wing choices, one nightrun graph, and one trader prove the concept.
- Utilities use connection, capacity, demand, priority, condition, backup, and failure state—not engineering-grade fluid or airflow simulation.
- Room Focus is a camera/presentation state over the same simulation, not a separate interior game.
- The seven-day slice has an authored incident spine with systemic variation, not a procedural narrative generator.

## Red-team findings and corrections

| Challenge | Finding | Correction now locked |
|---|---|---|
| Is it merely two existing games combined? | The initial ingredients are familiar and the pitch could collapse into “cutaway shelter plus harsh survival.” | Make railway reclamation and scarce signal attention causal systems. Every signature slice beat must change the station simulation; surface resemblance alone is insufficient. |
| Is base building genuinely central? | It would not be if rooms were passive timers or story gates. | Every day includes reclaim/build/operate/adapt decisions; layout affects travel, isolation, utilities, storm recovery, treatment access, and capacity. The slice must end with a visibly larger station. |
| Does the player have creative control? | An authored shell risks feeling like fixed unlocks. | Each reclaimed bay supports multiple compatible families, movable major equipment, modules, specialization, and later repurposing. East and West both permit distinct viable shelters. |
| Does the railway matter mechanically? | Names and tiles alone would be cosmetic. | Track drainage, platform edges, service trunks, signal infrastructure, vertical shafts, gates, and travel distance are construction constraints and opportunities. |
| Does the Listening Post affect ordinary play? | A separate signal inbox would become a text minigame. | Signals alter forecasts, unlock route knowledge, affect trader timing, reduce expedition uncertainty, warn of incidents, and supply ending evidence. Unfollowed signals have visible opportunity cost. |
| Are expeditions too abstract? | Delegation alone lacks spatial tension. | Both modes expose the same visible side-cutaway node graph, route, time, air protection, noise, carry limit, retreat point, and persistent location changes. |
| Are expeditions becoming a second complete game? | Fully direct control, combat, stealth AI, and many maps would overwhelm the shelter. | No joystick, one compact graph in the slice, brief decisions, reused interaction grammar, and a hard rule that each run answers a shelter objective. |
| Are residents too complex? | The requested profile could create unsupportable combinatorics. | Store rich character data, but surface it through a small set of authored thresholds, preferences, relationships, promises, and memories. No freeform runtime dialogue in launch scope. |
| Are utilities understandable on a phone? | Four simultaneous networks could be illegible. | Only one overlay at a time; each network reports source, path, load, headroom, condition, priority, and one plain-language failure cause. Exact readability requires a prototype. |
| Is there enough physical danger? | Avoiding combat could accidentally make danger abstract. | Storm damage, flooding, cinder exposure, heat, structural failure, illness, gate intrusion, and nightrun injuries remain visible and consequential, with preparation and recovery. |
| Is the visual target feasible? | Grounded 3D rooms, bespoke damage, and many residents are a major art and performance burden. | Use a modular station kit, shared resident rigs, layered materials/decals, a restricted side camera, authored damage states, and 10–14 normal residents. “Realistic” means material credibility, not photorealism. |
| Can a solo developer build the slice? | Only under severe content and tool limits. | One station segment, seven families, five characters, one expedition graph, an authored event spine, and target-device gates. A small team is more plausible for the full campaign. |
| Are there too many room families? | Twenty-four would already be difficult if each were unique. | The slice has seven; the full-game ceiling of 20–24 is not a target. New rooms must reuse a family or pass a unique-system acceptance test. |
| Does the campaign have a satisfying objective? | “Survive 45 days” alone is passive. | The player builds toward an evidence-backed Day 45 plan: evacuate, remain, or relay. Required preparation is telegraphed throughout the acts. |
| Can seven days prove the whole game? | It cannot prove late-game scale, only the risky core. | The slice proves building, legibility, human consequences, signal-to-action links, one expedition, and save/pause. It explicitly does not validate full-campaign content volume. |
| Is the design more impressive to read than to play? | This is the largest current uncertainty. | Prompt 2 must reduce the slice to a deterministic workload model; the next playable milestone must test tap-to-consequence speed, visible transformation, and recovery—not add lore. |

## Final concept decision

Proceed with **one authored railway interchange, flexible interior adaptation, a shelter-first simulation, scarce signal attention, and compact supporting nightruns**. The vertical slice is **The First Count**, a seven-day mini-campaign that must prove visible physical growth, understandable failure and recovery, meaningful East/West layouts, and one delayed human consequence.

This lock authorizes planning and prototypes only. It does **not** establish that Signal 45 is fun, balanced, production-ready, technically validated, or legally cleared.
