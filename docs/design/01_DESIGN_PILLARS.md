# Signal 45 — Design Pillars

**Purpose:** These pillars decide what belongs in Signal 45. A feature that does not support at least one pillar, or that violates an anti-pillar, is removed or redesigned.

## Pillar 1 — A Home Visible in the Cutaway

- **Meaning:** The station is both the main play space and the campaign record. Survival becomes emotionally meaningful because repair, habitation, damage, and care remain visible.
- **Player-facing behavior:** The player repeatedly zooms between the whole shelter and specific rooms, notices residents using what was built, and compares cold unusable areas with warm lived-in ones.
- **Supporting mechanics:** Reclaiming bays; room construction and repurposing; equipment placement; decoration; personal objects; utility lighting; visible damage and repair; population capacity; room travel.
- **Supporting presentation:** Predominantly side-on 2.5D cutaway; true 3D rooms and residents; cold-to-warm lighting progression; layered grime, repairs, laundry, signage, stored goods, and resident activity.
- **Concrete example:** A flooded west track bed becomes a drained cistern and workshop. Days later it shows pipe patches, labeled storage, a resident’s drying coat, and a backup lamp that activates during a storm.
- **Feature-rejection test:** Reject or redesign a shelter feature if its progress is represented primarily by a timer or menu number and produces no meaningful spatial, operational, or visual change.

## Pillar 2 — Pressure You Can Read

- **Meaning:** Survival is demanding because systems interact, not because the game hides causes or rolls arbitrary punishment.
- **Player-facing behavior:** The player reads forecasts, spots a weak link, changes priority or layout, accepts a declared risk, and can explain the result afterward.
- **Supporting mechanics:** Five stock resources; four utility networks; capacity and demand; condition; priority; backup; plain-language forecasts; incident warnings; Highball disclosures; staged failure; causal recap.
- **Supporting presentation:** One utility overlay at a time; symbols plus color; source-to-load paths; visible machinery state; forecast arrows; warnings with cause, time to consequence, and interventions.
- **Concrete example:** A cinder warning predicts filter saturation. The player can replace a filter, reduce workshop power, charge reserve cells, or knowingly risk poor air. If breathing injuries occur, the recap traces the missed intervention.
- **Feature-rejection test:** Reject any serious consequence whose material cause, warning, response window, and at least one practical intervention were not available to the player.

## Pillar 3 — People, Not Personnel

- **Meaning:** Residents are a small community with skills and agency, not collectible production modifiers.
- **Player-facing behavior:** The player assigns ordinary work, listens when danger crosses a boundary, keeps or breaks specific promises, accommodates recovery, and sees relationships alter willingness.
- **Supporting mechanics:** Four primary conditions; skills without overall levels; traits; preferences; hard and soft boundaries; relationship ties; authored memories; promises; negotiation; refusal with reasons; personal recovery behaviors.
- **Supporting presentation:** Named portraits; distinctive silhouettes and movement; contextual speech; relationship and promise callouts; visible rest, treatment, argument, volunteering, and reconciliation.
- **Concrete example:** After Teo completes a Highball repair, the player promises the next Slack phase off. Keeping it restores cooperation and shows Teo sleeping away from the generator; breaking it makes a later overtime request subject to negotiation or refusal.
- **Feature-rejection test:** Reject any resident system that can be reduced to rarity, one overall level, anonymous replacement, or a universal obedience percentage.

## Pillar 4 — Reclaim the Railway

- **Meaning:** Building works with an authored station rather than painting rooms onto a blank grid. Railway infrastructure provides constraints, opportunities, history, and identity.
- **Player-facing behavior:** The player surveys, clears, drains, reinforces, connects, builds, operates, and adapts different parts of the station, creating a layout with meaningful tradeoffs.
- **Supporting mechanics:** Authored bays; track drainage; columns and arches; stairs and lifts; service trunks; gates; access distance; room compatibility; utility branches; equipment footprints; upgrades; repurposing and deconstruction.
- **Supporting presentation:** Original railway geometry remains legible through new partitions and equipment; repaired signal colors and station wayfinding become the community’s interface language.
- **Concrete example:** East offers intact staff rooms near the sleeping area but far from the gate; West offers a damaged pump gallery near salvage. Either can hold several room families, but their infrastructure makes different plans efficient.
- **Feature-rejection test:** Reject a location, room, or upgrade if it could be moved unchanged into any generic bunker and lose no mechanical or presentational meaning.

## Pillar 5 — Listen, Prepare, Live With It

- **Meaning:** Information is scarce, uncertain, and actionable. Choosing what to learn is part of survival, and outcomes persist in the shelter and campaign.
- **Player-facing behavior:** The player commits Listening Post attention, compares confidence and expiry, acts on imperfect intelligence, and later sees which opportunities and warnings were missed or misunderstood.
- **Supporting mechanics:** Signal source, category, confidence, age, commitment, expiry, and possible value; limited listening capacity; forecasts; route discoveries; trader contacts; faction knowledge; expedition risk reduction; evidence ledger; ending requirements.
- **Supporting presentation:** Diegetic radio equipment; waveform/signal cards with plain-language confidence; map annotations; residents discussing relevant information; physical preparations appearing in rooms.
- **Concrete example:** Following a weak municipal burst reveals a west drainage release before the storm. The knowledge allows an early isolation valve project, but consumes the window in which a medical trader could have been located.
- **Feature-rejection test:** Reject a signal or narrative choice if it does not change an ordinary shelter decision, expedition, visitor, relationship, resource state, incident, or campaign outcome.

## Anti-pillar 1 — No Meaningless Room Spam

- **Rejected:** Dozens of rooms whose only distinction is icon, tier, or passive timer.
- **Why it harms Signal 45:** It weakens physical credibility, overloads mobile menus, inflates art and balance work, and makes layout cosmetic.
- **Prohibited behavior:** Building five nearly identical food rooms for linear output; merging rooms by an inherited genre rule; adding a bespoke room family for every crafting ingredient; unlocking a fixed “correct” room in each bay.
- **Acceptable nearby alternatives:** A Food family with temporary/permanent forms, equipment modules, staffing, recipes, and specializations; one Workshop family whose installed stations determine available crafts.

## Anti-pillar 2 — No Combat Power Curve

- **Rejected:** Routine combat as the main route to resources, stronger residents as fighters, weapon rarity treadmills, repeatable raids, and shooting mastery.
- **Why it harms Signal 45:** It displaces preparation, care, negotiation, and construction while multiplying animation, AI, control, and content scope.
- **Prohibited behavior:** DPS stats on residents; enemy waves tuned around gear score; loot runs whose optimal answer is killing; direct joystick shooting; combat rooms that generate power progression.
- **Acceptable nearby alternatives:** Barricades, locks, detection, route control, nonlethal tools, negotiation, retreat, brief last-resort force, injuries, and difficult aftermath.

## Anti-pillar 3 — No Invisible Random Punishment

- **Rejected:** Untelegraphed death, secret offline consumption, arbitrary refusal, opaque cascades, and failure designed to sell relief.
- **Why it harms Signal 45:** It destroys trust in the simulation and turns decisions into superstition.
- **Prohibited behavior:** A healthy resident dying from an unseen roll; a storm destroying an unmarked structure; water disappearing while the app is closed; a hidden percentage causing Highball catastrophe.
- **Acceptable nearby alternatives:** Uncertainty with labeled confidence ranges, visible risk factors, forecasts, escalating symptoms, response time, committed outcomes serialized before closure, and causal recaps.

## Anti-pillar 4 — No Human Loot Tiers

- **Rejected:** Resident rarity, gacha-style recruitment, one overall level, duplicate characters, and expendable optimal-worker logic.
- **Why it harms Signal 45:** It contradicts conditional authority and specific human consequence.
- **Prohibited behavior:** Legendary survivors; color-coded rarity; buying a better medic; sacrificing a low-tier resident because another has higher stats; universal XP that erases weaknesses.
- **Acceptable nearby alternatives:** Different skills and limitations; practice in specific work; equipment familiarity; trust earned in relationships; injuries and recovery; people who remain valuable for distinct reasons.

## Anti-pillar 5 — No Uncontrolled Scope or Asset Drift

- **Rejected:** Adding systems because a reference game has them, accepting inconsistent AI output as runtime art, and expanding content before the core slice works.
- **Why it harms Signal 45:** A solo or small team cannot polish divergent rooms, characters, maps, visual styles, and secondary games at the required mobile quality.
- **Prohibited behavior:** Forty independent rooms; several station maps before one is excellent; pets or vehicles without a proven core need; photoreal hero assets beside uncorrected generated props; runtime generated dialogue; feature work that bypasses prototype gates.
- **Acceptable nearby alternatives:** Reusable room families; modular kits; authored dialogue templates; concept-generation references with rights/provenance records; one excellent station; explicit post-launch gates.

## Feature acceptance tests

A proposed feature enters scope only when all applicable tests pass:

1. **Pillar support:** It materially supports at least one pillar and names the player behavior it creates.
2. **Shelter consequence:** If it is a signal, expedition, story event, or visitor, it changes normal shelter play or a campaign preparation.
3. **Spatial consequence:** If it is construction, it affects use, layout, travel, utilities, safety, capacity, or visible identity.
4. **Human legibility:** If it harms or compels a resident, it exposes cause, boundary, warning, and consequence.
5. **Mobile legibility:** Its common action fits contextual selection and does not require persistent UI or pixel-perfect targeting.
6. **Scope reuse:** It reuses a family, graph, rig, interaction grammar, or authoring pipeline unless a unique implementation is justified.
7. **Reference distance:** It has its own railway-native mechanical, visual, and narrative expression.
8. **Prototype gate:** Any unproven timing, performance, readability, or fun claim is labeled and assigned to a prototype.

## Feature rejection examples

| Proposal | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| “Rare” recruit packs with stronger base stats | Reject | Violates People, Not Personnel and monetization boundaries. |
| A kennel and pet-breeding tree at launch | Defer | Adds animation, AI, narrative, and UI without proving the shelter core. |
| A pump module that turns a track drain into water resilience | Accept for prototype | Railway-native, spatial, legible, and central to West’s route identity. |
| Ten-minute active stealth levels with direct movement | Reject for slice | Becomes a second game and breaks mobile-session scope. |
| A signal that only reveals lore text | Redesign | Must affect a forecast, relationship, route, visitor, evidence state, or preparation. |
| A cosmetic blanket made from found textiles and visibly used by Maren | Accept if cheap | Supports home transformation and character specificity without a new system. |
| Hidden 5% generator explosion under Highball | Reject | Serious risk is invisible; replace with labeled conditional breakdown risk. |
| Another unique crafting room for electronics | Reject | Install an electronics bench module in Workshop instead. |
