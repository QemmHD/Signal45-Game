## 1. Inspiration-principle matrix

Broad, unprotectable genre principles studied, and how Signal 45 expresses each originally:

| Reference principle (genre-level) | Commonly seen in | Signal 45's independent expression |
|---|---|---|
| Readable side-cutaway base | Shelter-management genre broadly | A *railway interchange* cutaway: platforms, stranded railcars-as-rooms, service vaults; visible cable/pipe dependency runs (Pillar 1) |
| Specialized functional rooms & base building | Shelter & colony sims | Reclamation construction (D-038): survey/clear/drain/reinforce/construct within an authored complex of sections and bays; footprint shells and module attachment instead of identical-room merging; railway-native room identities (Flywheel Room, Scrubber Gate, Listening Post, Sleeper Car) |
| Visible inhabitants doing believable work | Shelter & colony sims | 4–20 *named* people (ceiling test-gated, D-038) with values/Marks; posture-as-telemetry; refusal as a system |
| Matching people to responsibilities | Genre-wide | Aptitude *fit* + relationship pairing effects + values as hard lines — assignment is a personnel decision, not slot arithmetic |
| Internal incidents + external expeditions | Genre-wide | Incidents obey telegraph + 2-crisis cap; expeditions are short "door" sorties feeding the base, with full delegation path |
| Day/night phase rhythm | Civilian-survival games | Four *named* phases driven by decision completion (not wall clock); the planning phase (Slack) owns the signature radio ritual |
| Scarcity forcing prioritization | Survival genre | Twin-ledger scarcity: material scarcity is quoted; human cost is discovered later. Information itself is the scarcest resource |
| Crafting & shelter maintenance | Survival genre | Small purposeful catalog (12 slice items) chained to the week's actual problems (wire → antenna → Relay Night) |
| Difficult decisions with personal consequences | Narrative survival | Consequences post to named people as durable Marks and return physically at the gate |
| Randomized situations | Genre-wide | Authored event pools with schedule constraints; randomness in *which* and *when*, never in "whether you were warned" |
| Mobile return-friendly sessions | Mobile management | Phase-boundary safe stops; zero offline simulation; no re-engagement hooks (Anti-pillar 1) |
| Tone unity across art/audio/mechanics | 11-bit-school design | "Soot & lamplight" warmth-inside-dread identity, enforced by pillar tests |

## 3. Forbidden similarities (hard list, from the originality boundaries)

Never use or imitate: Vault-Tec or any corporate-vault fiction; nuclear-vault retrofuturism; blue-and-yellow jumpsuit iconography; bottle-cap or cap-like junk currency; S.P.E.C.I.A.L.-style acronym stats; wrist-device/Pip-Boy-like UI framing; any other game's room names, room-merge rules, or interface layout; existing characters, factions, dialogue, incidents, quests, icons, or lore; a charcoal-photocopy/newsprint monochrome treatment recognizable as This War of Mine's; direct recreation of a real, specific war scenario; existing moral encounters with renamed participants; another game's exact day/night structure; scraped, traced, or unlicensed assets (including unreviewed AI output — see 06, risk R-03).

**Metro-fiction additions (red-team, D-036):** no munition-based or ammunition-adjacent currency; no mutant or anomalous tunnel fauna; no station-name political ideologies mirroring Metro's faction pattern (station-states as ideological civilizations); no gas-mask-and-filter-timer survival horror staging as an identity image; no "surface run in a cracked mask" set-piece framing.

## 5. Visual-distinction rules (binding on the art stage)

1. **Architecture is testimony:** every space must read as *former public transit* — tile bands, platform edges, signage voids, stair mouths, the stranded train. No corridor-and-cell vault grammar.
2. **Palette law:** interiors live in tungsten amber/soot umber with teal-slate shadow; exteriors in ash-white/heat-haze; night in indigo/ember. **Forbidden:** the blue-and-yellow institutional pairing as any faction/uniform identity; full monochrome charcoal treatment.
3. **Character proportion:** stylized-realistic (~1:6.5 heads), silhouette-first, distinct color keys per resident. **Forbidden:** chibi/bobblehead proportions; photoreal.
4. **UI grammar:** flat, quiet UI with railway-artifact motifs (punch cards, chalk, gauges) *as motifs, not chrome*. **Forbidden:** wrist-device framing, terminal-green retrofuture styling, mascot iconography, any recognizable icon set.
5. **Damage/repair as three readable stages** with restoration visibly warming rooms — progression is *light returning to a station*, an image neither comparison title owns.
6. **AI-assisted concept output (Higgsfield) is reference-only upstream material**: production assets are rebuilt in the controlled modular pipeline; no raw generation ships; every shipped asset traceable to a human-directed source of truth (see risk R-03, R-13).

## 6. Narrative-distinction rules (binding on the narrative stage)

1. No real war, city, or disaster re-created under thin renaming; Harwick and the Char are composites of *climate-era* imagery, not a specific historical event.
2. No moral encounter may be a renamed version of a known scenario from another game; each must grow from Signal 45's own systems (radio triage, gate arrivals, filter economy, rail politics).
3. Factions must have economic logic native to this world (tidal power, tunnel trade, rail control) — no raider/paladin/cult stock trio.
4. The tone contract: catastrophe is climate-era and systemic, never gleeful post-apocalypse kitsch; humor exists but is human-scale (canteen banter, kiosk pedantry), never fourth-wall mascot humor.
5. Character voices, names, and biographies are original creations; no likeness of real persons.
