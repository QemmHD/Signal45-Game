# Signal 45 — Originality Boundaries

## Position

Signal 45 deliberately uses legible survival-management conventions. Originality must come from their railway-native transformation, the scarce-information loop, conditional human authority, visible physical adaptation, and a Day 45 objective assembled over time.

This audit is a design safeguard, **not legal clearance**. Recognizable similarity, trademark, trade dress, copyright, marketing comparison, and asset provenance require professional review before public announcement and again before release.

## Familiar-mechanic audit

### 1. Side-cutaway shelter overview

- **Why players understand it:** Multiple rooms, residents, and problems can be read simultaneously.
- **Signal 45 version:** An authored underground interchange shown as a predominantly side-on sliced map, with track beds, platforms, vertical shafts, gates, drainage, and service trunks remaining mechanically relevant.
- **Visual distinction:** Grounded true-3D railway materials, layered depth, cinder light, damp green-gray tunnels, emergency amber, signal-color accents, and inhabited warmth; no direct charcoal/monochrome treatment.
- **Mechanical distinction:** Overview and Room Focus are two camera states over one simulation. Infrastructure topology controls access, utility paths, travel, isolation, and compatibility.
- **Narrative distinction:** The cutaway is a record of a specific community reclaiming public infrastructure and deciding whether the station is refuge, terminal, or network node.
- **Remaining risk:** The broad “inhabitants visible in a cutaway base” silhouette is recognizable. Layout, proportions, UI placement, camera motion, room rhythm, and marketing imagery need comparative review.

### 2. Room construction and upgrading

- **Why players understand it:** Rooms turn strategic intent into visible, persistent capacity.
- **Signal 45 version:** Reclaim an existing bay, connect access/utilities, choose a family, place major equipment, then adapt with modules, specialization, movement, repurposing, or deconstruction.
- **Visual distinction:** Original railway shell remains visible; partitions and equipment look fitted around tile, arches, rails, columns, leaks, and old signage.
- **Mechanical distinction:** Seven slice families share logic across temporary/permanent forms. Layout changes travel, failure isolation, gate-to-clinic distance, network load, and storm recovery.
- **Narrative distinction:** Residents name, personalize, and remember spaces; a clinic or berth expresses whom the community chose to protect.
- **Remaining risk:** Rectangular room bays and tiered upgrades can resemble genre standards. Avoid copied dimensions, merge rules, upgrade timing, room lists, and icon grammar.

### 3. Reclamation and expansion

- **Why players understand it:** Clearing fogged or blocked space provides anticipation and long-term growth.
- **Signal 45 version:** Survey, clear, drain, reinforce, open access, and reconnect utilities within an authored interchange; horizontal and vertical routes have different infrastructure advantages.
- **Visual distinction:** Rubble, standing water, warped doors, collapsed stairs, cinder ingress, damaged conduits, and repair stages change the same geometry.
- **Mechanical distinction:** Expansion consumes work and detailed components, can expose hazards, and changes network topology. Reclaimed bays allow several viable uses rather than one predetermined room.
- **Narrative distinction:** Sections contain traces of the old station, signal evidence, and choices about what civic functions the new community values.
- **Remaining risk:** Linear “unlock next room” pacing would erase the distinction. Both first routes and later bay choices must remain functionally flexible.

### 4. Resident assignment

- **Why players understand it:** Matching people to work creates readable optimization and visible activity.
- **Signal 45 version:** Named residents bring specific skill, weakness, work preference, boundary, relationship, memory, and recovery behavior. Safe routine work is assignable; dangerous or boundary-crossing work may be negotiated or refused for stated reasons.
- **Visual distinction:** Believable proportions and shared production rigs with distinctive silhouettes, clothing layers, posture, tools, and personal routines rather than exaggerated collectible caricatures.
- **Mechanical distinction:** No rarity or overall level. Willingness depends on condition, protection, danger, promises, relationships, and trust-relevant history.
- **Narrative distinction:** The coordinator’s authority is on loan; staffing decisions become remembered treatment of particular people.
- **Remaining risk:** Portrait rails and skill indicators are common. Avoid copied SPECIAL-like stats, card frames, rarity colors, animation beats, and recruit presentation.

### 5. Stock resources and survival consumption

- **Why players understand it:** Food, water, medicine, materials, and energy reserves provide immediate planning language.
- **Signal 45 version:** Only five stocks remain on the main HUD; detailed component families explain blockers. Charge is reserve cell-hours, distinct from the connected Power utility.
- **Visual distinction:** Stocks appear through station-native storage, tanks, lockers, crates, flywheel/battery equipment, and plain original symbols.
- **Mechanical distinction:** Forecasts expose consumption, production, minimum reserve, and causal risks. No offline depletion and no monetized relief.
- **Narrative distinction:** Shortages affect meals, treatment, promises, visitors, and community choices, not anonymous population attrition.
- **Remaining risk:** Resource bars are universal but their exact arrangement, shapes, colors, formulas, and warning animations must be original.

### 6. Utility networks

- **Why players understand it:** Power/water/air capacity makes base layout and priority meaningful.
- **Signal 45 version:** Power, Air, Water, and Structure each use capacity, demand, priority, condition, connection, backup, and failure state; one overlay is visible at a time.
- **Visual distinction:** Restored station cable trays, pipes, drainage channels, fans, braces, valves, and signal lamps show actual paths.
- **Mechanical distinction:** Utilities are not currencies. Branch isolation, distance/topology, redundancy, overdraw, contamination, and cascading failure matter through bounded network abstractions.
- **Narrative distinction:** Shutting down one room to save another affects named residents, promises, and preparation.
- **Remaining risk:** Dense network simulators offer recognizable overlays. Use original iconography, plain failure sentences, restrained topology, and mobile-specific interaction.

### 7. Resident needs, injury, and recovery

- **Why players understand it:** Personal condition translates settlement pressure into human stakes.
- **Signal 45 version:** Exactly Health, Hunger, Fatigue, and Stress are primary meters; thirst, respiratory exposure, contamination, injury, grief, fear, and comfort are contextual states or memories.
- **Visual distinction:** Animation, posture, coughing, work pace, resting location, treatment props, and room use carry state without a forest of bars.
- **Mechanical distinction:** Serious harm follows warning, response time, understandable cause, available intervention, and sustained risk. Recovery behavior and accommodations matter.
- **Narrative distinction:** A treated person remembers who was overworked, protected, or denied rest, and this affects later cooperation.
- **Remaining risk:** Four bars can still feel gamey or familiar. Keep names plain, presentation restrained, and state change tied to observed behavior.

### 8. Crafting and equipment

- **Why players understand it:** Found items become purposeful improvements and preparation.
- **Signal 45 version:** A Workshop family gains mechanical, textile, electronics, or chemical capability through installed benches/modules. Equipment answers shelter projects, protection, repair, listening, and carrying needs.
- **Visual distinction:** Improvised railway tools, repaired municipal hardware, filter masks, patched bags, and labeled component drawers; no borrowed weapon silhouettes or rarity glow.
- **Mechanical distinction:** Recipes use bounded component families, tool access, resident skill, condition, and declared purpose. Equipment degrades or is repaired without a loot-score ladder.
- **Narrative distinction:** Personal equipment can carry provenance, ownership, promises, or reluctance to risk it.
- **Remaining risk:** Recipe grids and color tiers are highly recognizable. Avoid copied recipes, item names, slot layouts, upgrade trees, and scarcity formulas.

### 9. Incidents and failure cascades

- **Why players understand it:** Emergencies test preparation and break repetitive optimization.
- **Signal 45 version:** Forecastable physical chains such as cinder load → filter saturation → power overdraw → pump loss → water risk, with branch isolation and recovery actions.
- **Visual distinction:** Damage appears in geometry, light, particles, water, smoke, sparks, bracing, resident movement, and shutdown rooms.
- **Mechanical distinction:** Incidents have cause, stage, response clock, interventions, downstream effects, and a causal recap. Highball can trade time for known costs and labeled uncertainty.
- **Narrative distinction:** Aftermath creates memories, broken or kept promises, altered room use, and recovery scenes.
- **Remaining risk:** Random disaster cadence could resemble other survival games. Author original causes rooted in the station and avoid copied event wording or choice structures.

### 10. Day/night rhythm and expeditions

- **Why players understand it:** A repeating safe/dangerous rhythm makes planning and external action legible.
- **Signal 45 version:** Extreme day heat keeps work underground; Slack prepares a Nightrun window; Graymorn counts physical and human consequences.
- **Visual distinction:** Cinder-filled orange exterior day, heavy night darkness, headlamp cones, side-cutaway locations, and damp dawn return through the gate.
- **Mechanical distinction:** Active and delegated play use the same node graph, route, time, noise, visibility, air protection, carry, tools, hazards, retreat, and persistent site state—without joystick control.
- **Narrative distinction:** Runs pursue declared shelter needs or signal leads and return evidence, people, obligations, or consequences as well as items.
- **Remaining risk:** Scavenging at night is strongly associated with existing works. The shared graph, radio causality, heat/cinder logic, locations, UI, and encounter writing must be demonstrably distinct.

### 11. Difficult community decisions

- **Why players understand it:** Tradeoffs reveal values and create memorable consequences.
- **Signal 45 version:** Decisions are grounded in actual capacity, utilities, layout, risk, relationships, and promises; there is no global morality meter.
- **Visual distinction:** Consequences appear in occupied/empty berths, gate activity, treatment queues, room arguments, personal belongings, and later volunteer/refusal behavior.
- **Mechanical distinction:** More than binary good/evil options; logistical preparation can open alternatives, while consequences persist in specific state.
- **Narrative distinction:** The player is an accountable coordinator, not an omnipotent ruler, and residents may negotiate or refuse.
- **Remaining risk:** Harsh-choice presentation can become derivative or emotionally manipulative. Avoid copied dilemmas, phrasing, faction archetypes, and “suffering as spectacle.”

### 12. Listening Post and uncertain signals

- **Why players understand it:** A radio is a clear source of warnings, contacts, mysteries, and external opportunity.
- **Signal 45 version:** The player cannot follow every transmission. Each may show source, category, confidence, age, value, commitment, expiry, and uncertainty; attention changes shelter and campaign state.
- **Visual distinction:** A small improvised relay grows into a railway operations space with original signal diagrams, patch cables, notebooks, lamps, and antenna status.
- **Mechanical distinction:** Signal commitments compete with resident work and time, modify forecasts/routes/visitors/incidents, and add evidence to internally coherent scenario packages.
- **Narrative distinction:** Conflicting claims about an evacuation train frame the entire campaign, while mundane signals still save lives and shape relationships.
- **Remaining risk:** “Mysterious numbered signal” and radio-interface tropes are broad. The exact Signal 45 terminology, frequency fiction, audio motifs, UI, messages, and resolution need clearance and originality review.

### 13. Long-term progression and endings

- **Why players understand it:** Growing capacity and an approaching deadline create direction.
- **Signal 45 version:** Physical shelter stages, evidence, redundancy, social willingness, and ending-specific infrastructure converge on Day 45.
- **Visual distinction:** Ending preparations occupy real station space—packed berths, reinforced gates, relay arrays, platform access, stored travel kits, or community facilities.
- **Mechanical distinction:** Earned ending plans are evaluated from accumulated state. A final operational choice cannot substitute for missing preparation.
- **Narrative distinction:** The question is not simply whether the train is true, but what the community became while waiting and whether it still wants the offered future.
- **Remaining risk:** Deadline structures and multiple endings are common. The evidence chain and state evaluator must avoid recognizable borrowed beats.

## Forbidden similarities

Do not copy or closely trace:

- protected characters, factions, locations, lore, dialogue, quests, story events, endings, radio messages, room names, item names, or currencies;
- exact room dimensions, merging rules, upgrade formulas, production curves, population formulas, incident tables, trait/stat systems, or expedition-resolution formulas;
- interface layouts, portrait treatments, icon silhouettes, color schemes, notification motion, camera framing, or input flow from a reference title;
- charcoal rendering, monochrome treatment, character proportions, camera behavior, environmental compositions, or effects associated with a specific survival game;
- existing animation sets, maps, concept art compositions, promotional key art, sound motifs, music, or voice performance;
- recognizable AI-generated imitations of a living artist, a named game’s exact style, or an unlicensed asset.

## Visual boundaries

- The style is grounded and material-real, not photoreal and not a bright collectible cartoon.
- Cold damaged zones and warm inhabited zones form the progression palette; railway signal colors are functional accents, not a copied HUD palette.
- Characters use believable proportions, strong silhouettes, shared technical rigs, and individually authored clothing/behavior details.
- The camera remains side-on with selective physical depth and foreground fading. It does not reproduce another game’s exact composition, zoom, vignette, or cutaway rhythm.
- Higgsfield or other generative outputs may inform mood/reference only until consistency, rights, cleanup, modeling, rigging, animation, texturing, pivots, collisions, optimization, and export are documented.

## Mechanical boundaries

- Do not inherit formulas, room-merging conventions, recruit rarity, special-stat acronyms, timer cadence, loot tiers, energy monetization, or raid structures from reference games.
- Each familiar system must pass the “railway, scarce information, specific people” transformation test.
- A feature that only changes terminology while preserving a recognizable implementation pattern fails the audit.
- Comparative playtests should ask participants what the game reminds them of and why; repeated references to one title trigger presentation/mechanics review.

## Narrative boundaries

- All residents, factions, signals, dilemmas, terminology, and endings require an original canon and source/provenance record.
- Avoid one-to-one analogues of recognizable characters, scenarios, shelters, political structures, or moral dilemmas.
- Do not advertise Signal 45 through “Game A meets Game B” as its primary identity.
- The variable truth of Signal 45 uses authored, internally consistent packages; it is not a last-minute twist copied from mystery media.

## Remaining review risk

The highest recognizable-similarity risks are the side-cutaway shelter silhouette, night scavenging rhythm, harsh community-choice framing, and material-serious survival presentation. Mitigation is not avoidance of the genre; it is strong original geometry, railway-native topology, scarce-signal causality, distinct UI language, original authored content, documented references, provenance tracking, and professional review at concept-art lock and pre-marketing lock.
