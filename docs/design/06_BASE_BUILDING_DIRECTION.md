# Signal 45 — Base-Building Direction

## Building fantasy

> I found a dead railway station and slowly turned it into a living underground town.

Base building is the primary verb, the main source of long-term agency, and the physical record of the campaign. Signals, expeditions, visitors, relationships, and incidents feed construction choices; they do not replace them.

The station must visibly progress from emergency occupation to organized settlement:

- more reclaimed and connected space;
- different room purposes chosen by the player;
- improved and redundant utilities;
- safer routes and clearer work areas;
- more residents and appropriate capacity;
- personal possessions, comfort, decoration, and community identity;
- scars and repairs from remembered incidents.

## Starting shelter

The First Count begins in one damaged central platform zone with four provisional functional areas:

1. **Platform Camp — Rest family:** temporary bedrolls and a heat-screened corner; cramped, noisy, and insufficient for a fifth permanent resident.
2. **Relay Kiosk — Operations family:** an improvised Listening Post fitted into a damaged station booth.
3. **Service Alcove — Utility family:** limited flywheel/battery connection, improvised air fan/filter, and unreliable water pump access.
4. **Concession Cage — Storage/Food use:** secured stocks, cold ration preparation, and basic inventory staging; one temporary area serving two related uses, not two complete room systems.

Also visible but not functional: the main gate, blocked East and West passages, a jammed vertical access, flooded track drainage, rubble, broken lights, damaged trunks, and several reusable bays.

This starting state intentionally exposes inadequate sleep, water, air, structure, access, and treatment. It provides interventions rather than an unwinnable trap.

## Authored outer shell

The station owns the large-scale structure:

- platforms and track beds;
- tunnel walls, arches, columns, retaining walls, and floor elevation;
- major corridors, stairs, ladders, lift shafts, and surface entrances;
- drainage channels, sumps, utility trunks, and large fixed machinery;
- structural hazard zones and certain story locations;
- bay envelopes, access faces, and safe attachment points.

The shell makes the railway mechanically meaningful and gives art, navigation, camera, save, and incident systems bounded geometry. It is not a sequence of rooms with fixed labels.

## Flexible interior adaptation

Within compatible reclaimed bays, the player controls:

- room family and temporary/permanent form;
- partitions and permitted door positions;
- major equipment footprints and orientation;
- beds, workstations, storage, furniture, lighting, and decoration;
- utility modules, local backup, isolation, and priority;
- room specialization and operating policy;
- staff and task priority;
- movable equipment, stored equipment, upgrades, repurposing, and deconstruction.

Bay tags define understandable constraints such as dry/wet, ventilated, structurally limited, public/private, track-level, near-gate, or trunk-connected. Constraints should create tradeoffs and not a single correct answer. At least two room families should be plausible in most non-story bays.

## Physical and strategic layout consequences

Layout matters through a small set of legible consequences:

1. **Travel:** gate-to-clinic, storage-to-workshop, bed-to-work, and route-to-incident time.
2. **Networks:** connection length/topology, local capacity, bottlenecks, isolation, and backup reach.
3. **Exposure:** cinder ingress, damp, noise, heat, contamination, flooding, and structural zones.
4. **Adjacency:** select equipment or room forms gain clear logistical benefits, not opaque global bonuses.
5. **Access:** residents and responders need a valid safe path; blocked vertical or horizontal routes divide work.
6. **Capacity and privacy:** beds, treatment spaces, storage, and visitor holding must physically fit.
7. **Incident control:** doors, branches, pumps, braces, and alternate paths determine containment and recovery.

Avoid a web of hidden adjacency multipliers. When a consequence affects placement, preview it before confirmation.

## Expansion structure

### Reclaim a section

1. **Survey:** expose hazard, capacity, bay compatibility, likely salvage, access needs, and uncertain information.
2. **Make safe:** clear, drain, reinforce, remove cinder/contamination, or salvage unstable material.
3. **Connect access:** repair/open a passage, door, stair, ladder, lift, or temporary route.
4. **Connect utilities:** extend a branch or deliberately leave the section isolated.
5. **Build/adapt:** choose room purpose and equipment; make it operational.
6. **Inhabit:** staff it, supply it, observe use, then upgrade, specialize, personalize, or repurpose.

The player should see geometry and activity change at every stage.

### Horizontal growth

- East and West are the first meaningful route choice.
- Later wings provide alternate service access, civic/operations spaces, drainage, storage, and story locations.
- Long wings increase travel and network vulnerability, encouraging local backup and distributed functions.

### Vertical growth

- Restored stairs, ladders, and eventually a lift connect mezzanine, service, or surface-access levels.
- Vertical restoration is an access and utility project, not a generic unlock.
- The slice shows the jammed route and may survey it, but does not fully implement the later vertical game.

## Five construction workflows

The simulation may contain many verbs, but the phone interface exposes at most these five primary contextual workflows.

### 1. Reclaim

Includes Survey, Clear, Drain, Reinforce, Remove Hazard, and Salvage. On selection, the panel explains work, protection, component needs, salvage uncertainty, utility impact, and risk.

### 2. Connect

Includes Open Passage, Repair Door, Restore Stairs/Ladder/Lift, Extend Access, Connect Utility Node, and Isolate Damaged Branch. Connection previews show valid path and consequences.

### 3. Build

Includes Room Blueprint, Major Equipment, Furniture, Partition, Utility Module, valid Door, and Decoration. Placement supplies snap/rotate, large hit targets, Confirm, Cancel, and Undo.

### 4. Operate

Includes Staff, Priority, Repair, Supply, Maintain, Utility Priority, and Room Policy. It handles a functioning space without entering build mode.

### 5. Adapt

Includes Upgrade, Add Module, Specialize, Move, Store, Repurpose, and Deconstruct. It previews downtime, refunds/salvage, affected tasks, residents, networks, and irreversible loss.

Only actions valid for the selected section, room, object, project, or incident appear. Highball is offered contextually for an eligible project/repair/utility/emergency, not as a permanent sixth workflow.

## Slice room-family strategy

The slice uses **seven true room families**.

| Family | Temporary form | Permanent form / upgrade direction | Modules or specializations | Primary proof |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rest | Platform Camp | Bunkroom / Quiet Berths | Privacy screens, sound damping, extra berth, personal locker | Capacity, Fatigue recovery, newcomer readiness, home feeling. |
| Medical | Triage Cot/Bench | Clinic | Respiratory treatment, clean-water care, medicine cabinet | Injury/illness treatment and gate travel consequence. |
| Food | Cold Ration Table | Galley | Safe-water prep, heat plate, communal table, ration storage | Hunger forecast, water dependency, recovery/hope beat. |
| Storage | Secured Cage | Stores | Component drawers, food/medicine separation, expedition staging | Capacity, carry logistics, project blocker clarity. |
| Utility | Improvised Service Alcove | Machine/Pump/Filtration Room forms | Battery rack, filter bank, water treatment, isolation, monitoring | Power/Air/Water/Structure networks and storm cascade. |
| Workshop | Repair Mat/Tool Cart | Workshop | Mechanical, electronics, textile, chemical bench | Components, repair, filters, expedition equipment. |
| Operations | Relay Kiosk | Listening/Coordination Room | Better receiver, forecast board, route table, signal recorder | Scarce signal commitment and shelter-wide information. |

“Functional area” is not synonymous with room family. A temporary concession space may combine cold food handling and storage until the player builds a dedicated area. Major utility equipment specializations share Utility logic rather than becoming independent room systems.

## Upgrades, modules, and repurposing

- **Upgrade:** improves the room’s durable form and core capability while preserving family and identity.
- **Module:** adds a bounded capability, capacity, backup, or specialization with a visible footprint or equipment state.
- **Repurpose:** changes family within bay compatibility, with downtime, material recovery/loss, moved/stored equipment, resident/task displacement, and utility changes previewed.
- **Deconstruct:** removes player-built elements and recovers a declared portion; authored shell and story machinery remain.

The slice includes one upgrade and one adaptation/repurpose. Prompt 2 schedules Quiet Berths or Water Treatment as the route upgrade and the Concession repurpose as a shared proof, with exact provisional WU, material reservations, dependencies, target dates, and hard carryover dates in `tools/feasibility/data/model.json` and `12_WORK_AND_CONSTRUCTION_MODEL.md`.

## Utilities

Utilities are networks, not collectible currencies.

| Utility | Core question | Network elements | Representative failure | Recovery tools |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Power | Can connected equipment receive required output now? | Source, line, breaker/node, load, priority, condition, backup Charge | Overdraw or damaged branch shuts low priority loads and threatens air/water equipment. | Reprioritize, isolate, repair, add capacity, discharge Charge, stop a room. |
| Air | Can occupied spaces maintain breathable filtered air? | Intake, filter/fan, duct/zone, occupancy/load, contamination, seal | Cinder saturation reduces capacity and creates respiratory exposure. | Replace/repair filter, seal zone, reduce dusty work/occupancy, reroute, use protection. |
| Water | Can clean water be collected, treated, stored, and delivered? | Source/drain, pump, treatment, storage, line, demand, contamination | Pump loss or dirty intake reduces Clean Water and creates illness risk. | Isolate, boil/treat, repair pump, use reserve, ration, restore drainage. |
| Structure | Can the shell safely carry use and withstand incident load? | Zone rating, supports, damage, reinforcement, load/use, access | Cracks or collapse risk blocks a bay/route and may damage networks. | Evacuate zone, shore/reinforce, remove load, reroute, repair after incident. |

Every overlay uses the same information order: **source/state → connected path → demand/load → headroom → condition → priority/backup → current or forecast failure**. Only one overlay is active.

### Charge versus Power

- **Power** is connected real-time capacity, demand, topology, and condition.
- **Charge** is a stored stock used by battery/flywheel backup or portable cells.
- Charge does not replace generation and should not be consumed by every task merely as a stamina currency.
- Prompt 2 provisionally models Charge at 24 stored units within a 30-unit capacity, a 3-unit discharge limit per affected incident stage, and 10% losses. These remain prototype inputs, not final player-facing units.

## Population growth

- Start: four named residents.
- Slice possibility: admit Juna Malek as a fifth, temporarily support her externally, arrange shared shelter for a larger group, or refuse internal admission with specific alternatives/consequences.
- Full-campaign normal: approximately 10–14 residents.
- Technical validation target: approximately 16 residents plus representative visitors/incidents.
- Stretch: 20 only if performance, UI, animation, navigation, narrative, and room capacity all validate; no launch design depends on it.

Population requires physical berths, air/water headroom, food outlook, treatment access, work relevance, and human integration. Capacity is not a single abstract number even if the UI summarizes it.

## Shelter visual stages

### Stage 0 — Occupied ruin

Four improvised areas, cold darkness, visible rubble/flooding, exposed cables, scattered belongings, weak filters, and residents clustered on the platform.

### Stage 1 — Working refuge (end of slice)

Eight to ten functional areas, one reclaimed wing, separated treatment or food/storage use, extended utilities, a repaired route, stronger task lighting, labeled stores, first permanent partitions, personal berths or water resilience, storm repairs, and evidence of a communal meal.

### Stage 2 — Organized settlement (Act II)

Both major wings, vertical connection, specialized modules, redundant utilities, clearer public/private zones, more residents, trade staging, decoration, station wayfinding reclaimed as community identity, and visible incident scars.

### Stage 3 — Chosen future (Act III)

Ending preparations dominate selected spaces: a secured platform and travel staging, permanent civic/food/water capacity, a wider relay array, defensive storm works, or layered contingency systems. The station shows what the community intends to become.

## Base-building acceptance tests

1. An early and late slice screenshot is immediately distinguishable by rooms, light, people, organization, utilities, safety, and belongings.
2. Two players choosing the same route can plausibly place/adapt different compatible functions.
3. Two players choosing East versus West face different but viable forecasts and recovery tools.
4. Moving or repurposing a room can change travel, network, capacity, or incident response in a previewable way.
5. No slice room exists only as a passive timer.
6. Every major construction stage appears in the world and survives save/reload.
7. The player can complete common building actions on a phone without tiny taps, persistent menu coverage, or memorizing hidden bonuses.
8. The station remains railway-specific after player construction; partitions do not erase the authored shell.

## Prompt 3 survival/building amendment

Room function now depends on section-level Power, Air, Water, and Structure state. Isolation suspends the affected room; evacuation removes resident work; temporary conversion creates treatment/rest/storage capacity only by displacing the original function. Temporary bypasses and inferior substitutions create explicit later repair. Prompt 4 must place utility nodes and safe routes without adding freehand wiring or hidden survival WU.
