# 08 — DECISION LOG

Format per entry: **Decision · Reason · Alternatives rejected · Consequences · Assumptions · Revisit trigger.**

## Concept-lock initial pass

**D-001 — Preserve the seed's central promise verbatim; enforce it structurally.**
*Decision:* "Keep the station functioning without allowing the people inside it to lose their humanity" is the design's spine, enforced by the two-ledger model, twin failure axes, and Accord.
*Reason:* The promise already unifies fantasy, tone, and mechanics; the stage's job was enforcement, not invention.
*Alternatives rejected:* Recentering on the evacuation mystery alone (thriller framing) — weaker daily-loop connection; pure sandbox survival — discards the emotional contract.
*Consequences:* Every system must post to both ledgers or justify why not; failure design doubled (physical + human).
*Assumptions:* Two-axis failure is communicable to players. *Revisit trigger:* slice playtests show players don't perceive the human ledger as real stakes.

**D-002 — Signature hook is the Listening Post: information as a rationed, scheduled resource.**
*Decision:* Nightly zero-sum radio scheduling with expiry, off-screen resolution of the unheard, and a verification track for Signal 45.
*Reason:* Grows directly from the seed ("information is a strategic resource"); touches ordinary play daily (quality gate); no comparison title owns this verb.
*Alternatives rejected:* Hook = day/night expedition contrast (too close to an inspiration's signature identity to carry the game alone); hook = base-building depth (undifferentiated within the genre).
*Consequences:* Requires ~14 authored signals in the slice; radio UI is a first-class screen; weather telegraphing runs through intel.
*Assumptions:* Players enjoy allocating attention as much as allocating goods. *Revisit trigger:* playtesters treat radio night as a chore or always pick the same schedule.

**D-003 — Four named day-phases, decision-driven time, zero offline simulation.**
*Decision:* Swelter → Slack → Nightrun → Graymorn; phases advance on confirmation; the app closed = the world holds its breath.
*Reason:* Mobile safety gates (safe stops, no offline catastrophe), pause-native accessibility, and structural distinction from wall-clock survival games.
*Alternatives rejected:* Real-time day with speed controls (offline anxiety, reflex pressure); pure turn-per-day (loses phase rhythm and the night door).
*Consequences:* All tension must come from in-play scarcity and scheduling, not real-world timers; event scheduler works in phases, not minutes.
*Assumptions:* Decision-driven time still feels alive with ambient animation. *Revisit trigger:* playtests read the station as static/board-gamey.

**D-004 — Railway-native world: Kestrel Cross, Harwick, the Char, the Cinderline, filter-standard barter.**
*Decision:* All terminology, topology, and economy derive from the transit setting (04 §4 canon table).
*Reason:* Originality boundary compliance and thematic unity; the rail line doubles as campaign objective topology.
*Alternatives rejected:* Generic bunker/metro naming (adjacency risk to existing metro-fiction IP and genericism).
*Consequences:* Content writers are bound to the terminology table; tunnel topology replaces overworld maps.
*Assumptions:* Transit setting reads globally (stations are universal architecture). *Revisit trigger:* IP review flags metro-fiction adjacency (04 §7).

**D-005 — Small named cast; hard ceiling ~12; four in the slice.**
*Decision:* Anti-pillar 2 made structural: no anonymous population, ever.
*Reason:* The human ledger requires memory and specificity; content and animation budgets require a cap; mobile readability requires few agents.
*Alternatives rejected:* Growable population with named "key" residents atop a crowd (splits the design's heart); 8+ in the slice (content cost without proof value).
*Consequences:* No population-economy mechanics; arrivals are authored events; death is expensive content.
*Assumptions:* 4 people generate enough slice drama (the 20-event table says yes). *Revisit trigger:* slice feels under-populated in playtest ("empty station" notes).

**D-006 — No combat system, permanently.**
*Decision:* The run verb set is reach/take/talk/avoid/flee; violence exists as costly, rare, mostly off-screen consequence.
*Reason:* Anti-pillar 3 (identity), rating target, animation budget, and originality (the genre's combat is its most cloned part).
*Alternatives rejected:* Light combat (dominates player expectation and testing surface); auto-resolved combat stat-checks (invites power-fantasy tuning creep).
*Consequences:* Danger design must be environmental/social; some market expectation friction accepted and owned in store messaging.
*Assumptions:* Standoff scenes can carry threat convincingly. *Revisit trigger:* none for adding combat; revisit only how *threat* is staged if playtests find nights toothless.

**D-007 — Expeditions optional and 100% delegable; active mode built last.**
*Decision:* Delegated resolution is a first-class path with equivalent expected outcomes, richer than a stat roll (uses preparation, aptitude, intel, priorities).
*Reason:* Pillar 4, accessibility baseline, session flexibility, and the R-01/R-06 descope ladder (slice survives even if active mode slips).
*Alternatives rejected:* Mandatory active runs (accessibility failure, second-game risk); no active mode at all (loses the 3–6 minute intensity option the seed asks for).
*Consequences:* Outcome model must be shared between modes; active mode is presentation over the same resolution data.
*Assumptions:* Players won't perceive delegation as "the lesser game." *Revisit trigger:* playtest delegation-shaming or outcome divergence between modes.

**D-008 — Rooms are restored, not built; no free-form base layout.**
*Decision:* The station's spaces are authored; players choose what to restore, when, and how to configure function.
*Reason:* Readability (Pillar 1), modular art budget (C6), and originality (restoration-of-light identity vs. build-a-base genre default).
*Alternatives rejected:* Grid building (art/perf cost, vault-grammar adjacency); dig-out expansion (scope).
*Consequences:* Progression = restoration ladder; level design is authored per station section.
*Assumptions:* Configuration choice supplies enough agency absent layout choice. *Revisit trigger:* playtesters report no ownership feeling over the station.

**D-009 — Utilities are an abstract network (5 subsystems), not a routing simulation.**
*Decision:* Charge/water/air/heat/seals as connected states with visible dependency, three damage stages, telegraphed cascades.
*Reason:* Cascade drama without a second engineering game (C3); mobile screen budget; readability gate.
*Alternatives rejected:* Pipe/wire placement (C3); single "power" abstraction (too shallow to carry Pillar 1).
*Consequences:* Dependency must be drawn in art (cable runs) since it isn't built by players; tuning owns cascade fairness (R-08).
*Assumptions:* 5 subsystems is the readable maximum on a phone. *Revisit trigger:* R-08 warning signs (opaque or trivial cascades).

**D-010 — Business model: free Chapter One (Days 1–7) + one permanent unlock; the slice is chapter-shaped.**
*Decision:* Of the two permitted models, choose free-intro + single unlock; design the slice as exactly the free chapter's arc.
*Reason:* Mobile premium discovery is hard without a trial; the Day-7 Relay Night is a natural, honest cliff-of-meaning (not a manufactured paywall pain); double-uses the slice investment.
*Alternatives rejected:* Pure paid-up-front (discovery risk on mobile, weaker funnel); anything consumable (forbidden).
*Consequences:* Chapter One must be genuinely complete and generous; unlock placement is fixed at a hope beat, not a crisis (monetization must never touch the emotional premise — quality gate).
*Assumptions:* Trial-to-unlock converts adequately for this audience (unverified — see research notes). *Revisit trigger:* market research stage contradicts; user (project owner) preference differs. **[Blocking-question candidate #1 in the stage summary.]**

**D-011 — Slice = "The First Count," recommended targets adopted unchanged, two notes flagged.**
*Decision:* All recommended slice numbers adopted; Note A (finale counts as event #20) and Note B (one ending frame with state-reactive text) documented in 03 §3.
*Reason:* The targets are internally coherent and match the 7-day arc; changing numbers without production evidence would be guessing.
*Alternatives rejected:* Trimming to 3 survivors (kills the pair-dynamics proof); adding a second ending (content without new proof).
*Consequences:* 03 §3 enumerations are canon inputs to all later prompts.
*Assumptions:* 20 events suffice for 7 days without dead days (chain design says yes). *Revisit trigger:* slice playtest finds empty days or event pile-ups.

**D-012 — Twin failure axes, including the Toll Gate humanity-failure ending.**
*Decision:* Physical collapse (exodus) and human collapse (Toll Gate) are both authored failure endings with epilogues; individual deaths are wounds, not fail states.
*Reason:* Makes the central promise falsifiable in play; "station survives but shouldn't have" is the thesis stated as an ending.
*Alternatives rejected:* Game-over screens (cheapens the theme); unloseable narrative (removes stakes).
*Consequences:* Accord needs collapse-threshold content even in the slice (warning card); full game needs two failure epilogues.
*Assumptions:* Players accept failure-as-ending on mobile. *Revisit trigger:* rage-quit telemetry at failure vs. epilogue completion.

**D-013 — Engine selection deferred to next stage; requirements list maintained now.**
*Decision:* Per inputs; R-15 tracks it with a hard deadline at the systems/engine prompt.
*Reason:* Requirements (2.5D cutaway, ≤12 agents, save robustness, mid-tier mobile perf, modular pipeline) are now locked enough to drive selection honestly.
*Alternatives rejected:* Picking now for comfort (would smuggle tech constraints into concept).
*Consequences:* No implementation code this stage (working rule held).
*Assumptions:* Mainstream engines can hit the perf gate. *Revisit trigger:* R-15 deadline.

**D-014 — "Signal 45" is canon as the broadcast; commercial title decision deferred.**
*Decision:* Repository name (`Signal45-Game`) is honored by making Signal 45 the in-fiction broadcast; *Cinderline* remains the codename; title candidates (*Cinderline*, *Signal 45*) go to marketing stage with trademark search (04 §7 O3).
*Reason:* Alignment without premature branding commitment.
*Alternatives rejected:* Renaming the project now (no search done; out of stage scope).
*Consequences:* Docs use Cinderline; fiction uses Signal 45.
*Assumptions:* Either name survives search. *Revisit trigger:* marketing stage. **[Non-blocking question noted in summary.]**

**D-015 — Higgsfield is reference-only; production assets are rebuilt with provenance records.**
*Decision:* AI concept output feeds mood/anchors/keyframes/marketing only; the shipped modular kit is human-directed with per-asset provenance.
*Reason:* Consistency (R-03), IP hygiene (R-13, 04 §7 O4), and the quality gate barring dependence on raw AI output.
*Alternatives rejected:* AI-direct-to-production (inconsistency + provenance risk); no AI use (wastes a legitimate preproduction accelerator).
*Consequences:* Art budget must cover human production pass; provenance log from asset #1.
*Assumptions:* Tool terms permit commercial reference use (to verify — O4). *Revisit trigger:* legal review of tool terms.

**D-016 — Landscape orientation, exclusively.**
*Decision:* Per inputs; portrait permanently cut.
*Reason:* Horizontal cutaway composition; two-thumb interaction; one layout to perfect.
*Alternatives rejected:* Dual-orientation (doubles UI work, splits composition identity).
*Consequences:* All UI/readability gates assume landscape 6.1".
*Assumptions:* Audience accepts landscape (management-game norm). *Revisit trigger:* store-review friction signals at launch stage.

**D-017 — HUD carries exactly 5 resource indicators + day/phase clock; utilities live in the picture.**
*Decision:* Water/Rations/Charge/Salvage/Meds on HUD; subsystem states are read from the station itself (light, animation, sound) and confirmed on inspection.
*Reason:* The ≤6 gate; Pillar 1's world-first telemetry; density limits (09 A.9).
*Alternatives rejected:* Utility meters on HUD (double-encoding, density blowout); fewer resources via merging (Rations+Water) — loses distinct scarcity stories.
*Consequences:* Art must actually deliver telemetry-grade readability (blockout-testable, 07 §2/§4).
*Assumptions:* World-as-telemetry works on small screens. *Revisit trigger:* readability tests fail at blockout.

**D-018 — Accord is authority-on-loan: it gates the player's own verbs.**
*Decision:* Low Accord manifests as refusals, closed options, and withheld scenes — the player's power shrinks as trust erodes.
*Reason:* Makes the community meter *felt* rather than watched; distinct from genre morale bars (04 §2).
*Alternatives rejected:* Accord as lose-bar only; per-survivor obedience scores (micromanagement).
*Consequences:* Verb-gating must always explain itself (readability); slice needs both warm and withheld variants of key scenes.
*Assumptions:* Players read refusal as fair when caused. *Revisit trigger:* playtests read refusals as bugs or dice.

**D-019 — Telegraph law and the two-crisis cap are scheduler-enforced, not editorial habits.**
*Decision:* The event system structurally cannot fire an untelegraphed catastrophe or a third simultaneous crisis (tested, 07 §13).
*Reason:* Pillar 5 and the offline-safety/fairness gates need enforcement, not intention.
*Alternatives rejected:* Author discipline alone (drifts under content pressure).
*Consequences:* Event schema carries telegraph and severity fields from day one.
*Assumptions:* Caps don't flatten drama (chains supply compound tension within the cap). *Revisit trigger:* slice playtests feel under-pressured at Act-1 tuning.

## Research-integration amendments

**D-021 — Platform-guidance refinements adopted from research (05, Track 7).**
*Decision:* (a) Unified touch spec: primary targets ≥ 48 dp/pt with ≥ 8 dp spacing, secondary chrome ≥ 28 pt. (b) Text scaling: reading surfaces to 200%, dense HUD floors at 130%, body-text contrast ≥ 4.5:1. (c) Notification permission requested only when the player first schedules a reminder, framed diegetically at the Listening Post; denial costs nothing.
*Reason:* Verified Apple HIG and Android quality guidance (05, Track 7) set stricter or more specific bars than the draft's "platform minimums" phrasing; adopting the stricter bar as one spec removes per-platform ambiguity for later stages.
*Alternatives rejected:* Per-platform dual specs (double the audit surface); deferring to the UI stage (would let screens be designed against the looser bar).
*Consequences:* 07 §17/§19 and 09 A.6/A.7 updated; density budgets in 09 A.9 must be validated against 200% reading-text scaling at the UI stage.
*Assumptions:* 200% scaling is achievable on reading surfaces given the one-decision-at-a-time card design. *Revisit trigger:* UI-stage layout audit.
