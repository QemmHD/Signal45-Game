**Status:** LOCKED at concept level. This file carries primary objectives 9 (mobile experience) and 10 (visual identity); it is referenced by, and consistent with, 01/02/03/07. Final art prompts and asset specs belong to a later stage.

## Part A — The mobile experience

### A.1 Orientation
Landscape, exclusively. The cutaway station is a horizontal composition; landscape also matches the two-thumb interaction model. Portrait is permanently cut (03 §6). Safe-area insets (notches, home indicators) respected by keeping all interactive UI inside platform safe zones.

### A.2 Session length
Designed sessions of 5–12 minutes covering one to two day-phases; a full day fits a 12-minute session. Optional active nightruns are 3–6 minutes and always substitutable by delegation. No content requires a longer continuous sitting; the campaign is completable entirely in 5-minute sittings.

### A.3 Safe stopping points
Every phase boundary (4/day) is an autosaving, marked safe stop; every confirmed decision is durable; the nightrun launch is an explicit opt-in gate. The session loop deliberately ends on the exhale (day report / phase preview), so the natural stopping point is also the emotional rest point (Pillar 5).

### A.4 Pause behavior
The base game is pause-native: nothing in Swelter/Slack/Graymorn advances without player confirmation, and in-phase ambient simulation pauses freely. Active nightruns hard-pause on interruption (call, app switch, notification shade) and offer resume-or-delegate on return. There is no scenario in which the OS taking focus costs the player anything.

### A.5 Offline-progress philosophy
**None, in either direction.** No offline decay, no offline yield, no catch-up mechanics, no real-time appointments. Absence is consequence-free by design (Anti-pillar 1; quality gate). The fiction supports it: the stationmaster's log resumes where it left off.

### A.6 Touch interaction philosophy
- **Tap to inspect, tap to commit; drag only for assignment** (survivor → room), with a tap-tap alternative for every drag.
- One-thumb operable in base mode; pinch-zoom has a button alternative (07 §17).
- Touch targets ≥ platform minimums (~44 pt); primary actions live in thumb-reach corners; destructive/irreversible actions always confirm.
- No gesture vocabulary beyond tap / drag / pinch — nothing hidden behind long-press-only or multi-finger input.
- The 30-second loop (02 §2) is the interaction contract: notice → inspect (1 tap) → decide (≤ 4 options) → commit (1 confirming tap) → feedback.

### A.7 Notification philosophy
Opt-in only, player-scheduled only ("remind me tomorrow evening"), and never generated by game state — because game state cannot change while away (A.5). No re-engagement, streak, or FOMO notifications, ever. Default is zero notifications.

### A.8 Accessibility commitments (baseline, tested in 07 §19)
Scalable text to 130% minimum; color-safe palette with redundant shape/icon channels; captions for all audio including alarm identities; fully playable muted; no reflex requirement in base mode and a 1.5 s reaction floor plus full delegation path for runs; reduced-motion mode; left/right-handed layout mirroring in the full game.

### A.9 Information-density limits
- HUD: **5 resource indicators + day/phase clock** (within the ≤ 6 gate). Utility states live in the station picture, not the HUD.
- Max **3 alert badges** visible simultaneously; the event scheduler queues the rest (and may never exceed 2 active crises).
- Decision cards present **one decision at a time**, with 2–4 options each.
- Any single screen answers one question; anything needing two screens of explanation gets redesigned or cut (readability gate T1).

## Part B — Visual identity (concept level)

Working name for the identity: **"soot and lamplight."** Warmth is earned light inside a cold ruin; the station heals visibly as the player restores it.

### B.1 Camera and composition
Side-cutaway 2.5D, orthographic-leaning with shallow parallax depth (3–4 layers). Two working zooms: **overview** (the whole three-level slice section composes on one landscape screen: concourse above, platforms mid, service vaults below) and **room zoom** (one room fills ~60% of frame for scenes and panels). Nightrun locations use the identical camera language — the ruins are a dark mirror of home.

### B.2 Environment treatment
Modular 3D kit rendered stylized (hand-painted texture feel, controlled graphic edges — no photorealism, no outline-cartoon). Every module must testify to *former public transit*: tile bands, enamel signage voids, platform edge stripes, cable trays, the stranded train whose cars are now rooms. Damage reads in the architecture (buckled panels, water stains, soot shadows), not in decals alone.

### B.3 Character proportion
Stylized-realistic at ~1:6.5 head ratio; silhouette-first design with one distinguishing shape + one color key per resident (Imka's high-collar work coat, Ash's sling bag, Teo's runner's wrap, Maren's kiosk apron). Readable at overview distance by silhouette and gait alone. Forbidden: chibi/bobblehead proportions, photoreal likenesses.

### B.4 Lighting language
Lighting *is* the interface of hope: tungsten amber pools where the station lives, teal-slate darkness where it doesn't yet, ember/indigo for the surface at night, bleached ash-white heat-haze for the lethal day. Restoration = light returning, room by room (each restored room permanently warms). Accord's state tints the common spaces (warm/neutral/cold) rather than showing a meter face. Alarms shift light color *locally* at the failing subsystem first — light telegraphs before UI does.

### B.5 Material language
Soot, enamel, riveted steel, worn brass, waxed canvas, chalk. Repairs are visible patches: weld seams, stitched canvas, wire splints — the jury-rigged stage must *look* jury-rigged. Water is precious and always rendered carefully (drips, condensation on the cistern). Cinderfall is matte, dry, and hot-edged — never snow-like.

### B.6 Palette logic
| Context | Palette | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Lived interior | Tungsten amber, soot umber, teal-slate shadow | Earned warmth |
| Unrestored interior | Teal-slate, cold gray-green | Work remaining |
| Surface day (unseen except through seals/screens) | Bleached ash-white, heat-shimmer orange | Lethality |
| Surface night (runs) | Deep indigo, ember orange accents, lantern pool | Fragile opportunity |
| UI | Bone-white line work on smoked glass panels, chalk accents | Quiet, diegetic-adjacent |
Forbidden pairings: institutional blue-and-yellow as any identity; full charcoal monochrome (04 §5).

### B.7 Interactable readability
One consistent grammar: interactable objects carry a **brass-and-chalk** treatment (worn-bright edges, chalk tally marks by the door of every workable room) plus a soft breathing highlight at inspection range. Never floating icons over the world at overview zoom; badges live on the room's chalkboard plate. In runs, the lantern's light radius is the interaction radius — literal illumination as affordance.

### B.8 Damage and repair progression
Three stages per room/subsystem, silhouette-distinct: **failing** (dark, leaking, hazard-marked), **jury-rigged** (patched materials, flickering half-light, audible strain), **restored** (warm steady light, clean animation loop, resident-added domestic details). Domestic detail accrues with occupation over time — the station slowly becomes a home in the background layer, which is the visual proof of the whole premise.

### B.9 UI relationship to the world
UI is a thin, quiet layer styled after railway artifacts (punch cards, chalkboards, gauge needles) as *motifs*, rendered flat and modern for readability — diegetic-adjacent, not diegetic-literal. The world carries state (light, posture, drips); the UI carries decisions (cards, ledgers, schedules). When both could show something, the world shows it first and the UI confirms it on inspection.

### B.10 What must not resemble the main inspirations
No vault-door imagery, numbered-vault branding, uniform-suited population, mascot figures, or retrofuture terminal chrome; no charcoal-photocopy monochrome treatment, scrawled-diary UI, or war-reportage framing. The squint test (04 §7 O1): at thumbnail size, Cinderline must read as *warm light in a train station underground*, not as any existing shelter game. This is an art-stage acceptance gate.

### B.11 Production note (binding)
All of Part B is achieved through the controlled modular pipeline: Higgsfield output is upstream reference only (mood, anchors, keyframes, marketing exploration); every shipped asset is authored/rebuilt in the production pipeline with a provenance record (R-03, R-13). The visual identity must survive blockout: lighting language and silhouette readability are testable before any final texture exists, and 07's readability gates run on blockout art.
