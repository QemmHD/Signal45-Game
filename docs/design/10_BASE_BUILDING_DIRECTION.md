# 10 — BASE BUILDING DIRECTION

**Status:** LOCKED at concept level (revision pass, D-038). This document is the authority on construction, expansion, and shelter growth in **Signal 45**. It supersedes the earlier "restoration only / no free-form building" direction (D-008), which is formally retired in the decision log. Where this document and earlier documents conflict, this document wins; the affected documents have been revised to match.

**The correction this document encodes:** the first originality pass overcorrected. Removing base building removed part of the intended game. Signal 45 differentiates through *original implementation* of strong genre mechanics — railway-native reclamation, two-ledger construction consequence, information-gated expansion — not through the absence of building.

---

## 1. The building fantasy

> **"I found a dead railway station and slowly turned it into a living underground town."**

The player begins with a huddle of survivors camped on one damaged platform and ends — across the full campaign — with a reclaimed interchange: lit concourses, working farms on old platforms, converted railcars with curtains in the windows, a memorial wall by the stairs, and neighborhoods with names. Base growth must be **emotionally rewarding as well as mechanically useful**: every reclaimed section is light returning, and the station's transformation *is* the visible biography of the community.

The player regularly sees concrete evidence of progress: dark areas gaining electricity; rubble disappearing; flooded tunnels draining; bedrolls becoming bunks becoming private berths; an improvised worktable becoming the Fitters' Shop; bare concrete gaining insulation, paint, and murals; empty platforms becoming farms, clinics, kitchens, dormitories, and gathering spaces; new residents occupying areas that were sealed a week ago; broken railcars becoming rooms; and signs, plants, memorials, and personal belongings accumulating without being purchased or placed by the player — the residents decorate what they trust.

## 2. The station as an authored complex: sections and bays

Signal 45 does **not** hand the player an unlimited blank grid, and it does not confine them to a fixed layout. The middle path is **reclamation with real layout freedom inside it**:

- **Kestrel Cross is an authored complex** of ~14 candidate **sections**: platforms, concourses, maintenance corridors, service rooms, shafts, tunnel mouths, stranded rolling stock (see §14). A campaign seed selects and conditions a subset, so the complex varies between playthroughs.
- Each section is subdivided into **structural bays** — the span between roof columns (~6 m), the atomic unit of construction. Sections contain 3–10 bays plus fixed architectural features (stair mouths, track beds, cable galleries, the cistern main).
- **Expansion = reclaiming sections; layout = deciding what happens inside them.** Which section to open first, which bays become which rooms, what footprint each room takes, where access and utility connections run, what to preserve versus rebuild — all player decisions.
- The blank-grid infinite dig-out remains cut (03 §6, revised): expansion is bounded by the authored complex because the *found-architecture identity* — reclaiming a real place with a past — is the originality claim. Within those bounds, building is deep, frequent, and central.

## 3. The reclamation loop

The canonical expansion sequence — each step a real decision, not a menu formality:

1. **Discover/Survey** an inaccessible section (sight lines, a runner's report, radio intelligence, faction tips, old station schematics found on nightruns).
2. **Learn** its opportunities and hazards — surveys return a readable *section report*: what's in there, what's wrong, what it would take.
3. **Clear** the obstacle: rubble, standing water, contamination, cinder infiltration, wrecked machinery — via works orders, equipment, and sometimes nightrun-sourced tools.
4. **Reinforce** where the structure demands it (props, welds, new supports) — skipping this is possible and dangerous.
5. **Connect** access: stairs, corridors, ladders, the freight lift — access shape determines travel time and storm isolation forever after.
6. **Extend utilities** to the section's service node from the nearest trunk.
7. **Construct** a room shell in chosen bays, or adapt found architecture (a railcar, a kiosk row, a signal box).
8. **Furnish** it with functional equipment (this is where a shell becomes a kitchen rather than a room-shaped hole).
9. **Staff** it through the normal assignment system.
10. **Upgrade, specialize, decorate, or repurpose** it over time.

This loop threads through the existing day structure: surveys and works orders are Swelter decisions; radio-sourced section intelligence is a Slack/Listening Post decision; tools and schematics come home through the Nightrun door; sealed storm days become *building days* (surface locked, hands available — construction is the sanctioned storm-day activity).

## 4. Construction verbs (16)

Every verb has a mechanical meaning and a visible result. None reduces to a button plus a passive timer: works orders are staged, worker-performed, interruptible, and their stages are visible in the world.

| Verb | Mechanical meaning | Visible result |
|---|---|---|
| **Survey** | Spend labor (or intel) to reveal a section report: contents, hazards, costs, story hooks | Lantern sweep; chalk survey marks; the section renders in "surveyed" ghost detail |
| **Clear** | Staged removal of rubble/debris; yields salvage; opens bays | Rubble piles shrink stage by stage; dust; carried baskets |
| **Drain** | Pump-down of flooded bays; needs pump capacity + power; yields water if filtered | Waterline visibly falls; drips; sheen on newly exposed floor |
| **Reinforce** | Raise a bay's structural rating; prerequisite for heavy rooms and tall shells | Props, welded ribs, fresh plates against old cracks |
| **Excavate** | Rare, bounded: open a *known* collapsed passage or widen an access mouth (never free digging into virgin earth) | Broken wall becomes doorway; spoil heap accumulates |
| **Connect** | Build/repair stairs, ladders, lifts, corridor doors; changes the travel graph and storm-isolation options | New stair/ladder geometry; door states |
| **Construct** | Erect a room shell in 1–3 bays (or adapt found architecture) | Framing → walls → lit shell, in visible stages |
| **Furnish** | Install the equipment that gives a shell its function | Equipment appears piece by piece; the room starts *working* |
| **Staff** | Assign residents (existing assignment system) | People visibly working; the room's chalk plate gains names |
| **Upgrade** | Improve a room's tier: capacity, efficiency, comfort, safety | Better equipment, brighter light, tidier space |
| **Specialize** | Commit a room to a sub-role (clinic → surgery vs. ward; workshop → electronics vs. tools) | Distinct equipment silhouette and signage |
| **Repair** | Restore damaged rooms/equipment/structure (existing system, now one verb among sixteen) | Damage stages regress; patches visible |
| **Decorate** | Player-directed comfort/identity investment; residents also decorate autonomously as Accord and tenure grow | Paint, plants, murals, curtains, personal objects |
| **Expand** | Attach a module to an existing room (pantry to kitchen, ward bay to clinic) rather than rebuilding | The room visibly grows into the adjacent bay |
| **Repurpose** | Convert a room to another function, keeping the shell; partial refund of furnishings | Equipment swapped out; signage repainted over the old name |
| **Deconstruct** | Remove a room or equipment; **stores** major equipment for reuse and refunds a fair share of materials | The space returns to shell state; crates carried to storage |

**Anti-tedium guarantees (Anti-pillar 4 still applies):** construction decisions are made at works-order level, never per-survivor-footstep; standing orders exist for repeated jobs; the verbs above are *choices with consequences*, and their execution is watchable, skippable work — the player is never asked to babysit a progress bar.

## 5. Room footprints and shells

Rooms occupy different numbers and shapes of bays. Supported footprint classes:

- **1-bay utility rooms** (pump room, battery bank, guard post)
- **2-bay standard rooms** (dormitory, clinic, workshop)
- **3-bay major facilities** (hydroponics hall, dining hall, generator room)
- **Tall rooms** spanning two levels (the Flywheel Room's rebuilt regenerative rig, ventilation control around a shaft)
- **Narrow corridor facilities** (equipment lockers, filtration inline units, storage runs)
- **Converted railcar interiors** (sleeper car, aid car, private quarters — found architecture with fixed charming constraints)
- **Platform-spanning community spaces** (dining hall along the platform edge, market/trade row)
- **Feature-built rooms** constructed around existing environmental features (cistern main → water purification; signal box → Listening Post; stair mouth → trade entrance)

**Originality rule (04 §3):** Signal 45 has **no adjacent-identical-room merge rule**. Larger facilities come from (a) constructing a larger shell to begin with, or (b) **attaching modules** to an existing room via the Expand verb (kitchen + pantry module; clinic + ward module; dormitory + washroom module). Three small kitchens never fuse into a big one — and a big dining hall is a different design than three canteens, with different comfort, noise, staffing, and travel properties.

## 7. Room functionality model

Rooms are never passive resource boxes. Every room definition draws from this functional vocabulary: survivor assignments · installed equipment · storage capacity · utility consumption *and* production · noise emission · heat emission · air-quality effects · cleanliness state · comfort contribution · injury/incident risk · task generation (rooms create work, not just output) · travel-distance sensitivity · neighbor bonuses and penalties · staffing requirements · maintenance load · special events · visible resident activity.

**Degraded-operation rule:** a room without power (or water, or air) does not simply switch off uniformly — per-room definitions choose among *reduced capacity* (galley cooks cold meals), *unusable* (electronics bench), or *manual-labor fallback* (hand-pumping the cistern at brutal Fatigue cost). A damaged room visibly and mechanically changes (existing three damage stages apply per room).

## 8. Utility networks: trunks, nodes, and the "why is it offline" answer

Expansion interacts with **electricity, clean water, ventilation, heat, sanitation, structural integrity, and access/travel routes** — without ever asking a phone player to draw individual pipes or wires. *(Prompt-3 slice lock, D-043: the slice's live utility families are four — **Power, Air, Water-network, Structure** (18); thermal pressure is a room/incident modifier and sanitation derives from water + cleaning + crowding; both become independent networks in the full game only if testing proves the decisions justify the interface cost — 16 §1.)*

**The model (extends the existing abstract network + charge load-priority board):**
- **Trunks** run through the station's real cable galleries and duct runs (visible in the architecture, per Pillar 1).
- Each section has a **service node**; reclaiming a section includes connecting its node to a trunk (a works order, not a routing puzzle).
- **Rooms declare requirements** (power draw, water, air changes, heat); nodes and trunks have **capacity**; the load-priority board sets priorities; **emergency shutoffs** isolate sections (storm play).
- **Inspection always answers causally.** Tapping a non-functioning room states the reason in one panel, in plain language, with the failing link highlighted along the actual trunk path.

**Worked example (canon):** a new Hydroponics Hall on the east platform is structurally complete but offline: *"East branch node: power sufficient, water pressure 40% — pump room capacity exceeded."* The player's real options: extend the west trunk east; upgrade the Pump Room; build a local Battery Bank and schedule pumping off-peak; shut down the lower-priority Reclaim Room; or assign manual water-carrying as a stopgap (Fatigue cost). Five answers, different ledgers.

## 9. Layout strategy — and the right to be wrong

**There is no universally correct layout.** The layout dimensions that create strategy:

- **Compact vs. spread:** a tight core is utility-cheap and travel-fast but cascade-prone (fire, contamination, one trunk failure hits everything) and loud; a spread station is resilient and quiet but costs more trunk capacity, materials, and travel time.
- **Noise adjacency:** the Flywheel Room, pumps, and workshops emit noise; dormitories and the clinic suffer beside them. Cable distance argues for closeness; sleep argues for distance.
- **Vulnerability placement:** where the sick, the young, and the storm refuge sit relative to the Scrubber Gate, the flood line, and structural weak points.
- **Access shape:** which rooms need to be near storage, the gate, the galley; stair/lift placement writes the travel graph.
- **Storm isolation:** bulkhead doors and shutoffs let a well-planned station seal sections during cinderfall; a badly-shaped station chooses between heating everything or evacuating bays.
- **Preserve vs. rebuild:** found architecture (railcars, kiosk rows, the signal box) is cheaper and characterful but constrained; rebuilt shells are flexible but cost materials and erase history — sometimes history residents care about (human ledger).

**Recovery guarantees (a poor layout is a story, not a grave) — two-sided by design:** Repurpose keeps the shell and refunds part of the furnishings; Deconstruct stores major equipment intact and refunds a fair material share; moving a room = deconstruct + reconstruct at mostly-refunded cost, priced in labor and disruption rather than punitive material loss. The game never strands a player permanently in a Day-3 mistake — it charges them time, comfort, and a grumbling household instead. **And the charge is real:** every repurpose/deconstruct costs person-phases and produces at least one visible disruption or human-ledger echo (a bark, a Strain tick, a day-report line) — free thrash-relayouting is the opposite failure mode and is tested against (07 §21, R-22). Each authored slice layout consequence names its in-slice recovery verb (noise adjacency → re-site beds as a furnish-level order or swap the railcars' roles; overdrawn node → the ≥2 documented capacity responses).

## 10. Expansion constraints (believable, visible, multi-solution)

Expansion is limited by what is *physically and socially true* of each section, never by arbitrary level locks: rubble · flooding · fire damage · unstable ceilings · collapsed access routes · contamination · cinder infiltration · broken ventilation · missing structural support · insufficient materials · insufficient labor · insufficient utility capacity · missing survivor skill (a reinforcement weld needs a Fitter) · missing tools (nightrun-sourced) · missing knowledge (radio intelligence, schematics) · hostile or fearful occupants · faction agreements (the Tidemill claims salvage rights in the freight connection).

**Multi-solution rule:** flagship obstacles have ≥2 answers with different ledger costs. The flooded west gallery can be drained (pump capacity + time), or partially planked for access-only (fast, no water yield, ongoing damp), or negotiated (the Tidemill's pump barge, for a price). The civil-defense annex's sealed door can be cut (tools + noise + time) or opened with the key code from a verified radio thread (the Listening Post reaches into construction).

## 11. Population growth (restored)

Population growth is an important progression system. The slice holds 4 residents and **prepares a berth for a fifth**; the full game supports approximately **12–20 named residents**, with the exact ceiling subject to technical testing (R-04/R-05 gates — the number is a promise to *test*, not yet to ship).

- **Arrival channels:** rescue (distress calls answered), radio contact, faction agreements, visitors requesting shelter, family reunification (Teo's thread class), expedition discoveries, and people who were helped once and come back.
- **Growth creates requirements:** berths, food, water, sanitation, privacy, medical capacity, work assignments, social space, security, and — past ~10 residents — **governance** (the Meeting Hall class of rooms; disputes stop being two-person events).
- **Everyone stays a person (Anti-pillar 2 holds at the new ceiling):** every resident keeps traits, needs, values, relationships, and Marks. No anonymous migrant pools, no breeding economies, no population spam. Arrivals are authored characters at a controlled rate; the player can decline arrivals (a human-ledger decision with witnesses).
- Housing quality is felt: bedroll → bunk → private berth is a comfort/Strain/privacy ladder, and *who gets the first private berth* is exactly the kind of fairness decision the human ledger exists for.

## 12. Construction posts to both ledgers

Every construction decision quotes its physical price up front and discovers its human price later, like every other decision in Signal 45:

**Physical ledger:** production, storage, utility capacity, travel time, structural safety, defense, maintenance load, resource efficiency.
**Human ledger:** privacy, comfort, noise exposure, trust, belonging, fairness, grief, community identity, conflict, hope.

**Canon examples:** converting the Memorial Wall's bay into storage solves a supply crisis and wounds everyone who chalked a name on it (Accord loss, Marks for the bereaved); building Private Berths improves rest but spends bays the Mushroom Farm wanted, and *who gets them first* is a fairness event; siting the Flywheel beside the Sleeper Car shortens cable runs and ruins sleep (visible: residents relocating bedrolls, grumbling barks) until re-sited; opening the sealed east platform reveals working machinery *and* the belongings of the people who sheltered there during the evacuation — including, in some seeds, evidence tied to a resident's missing family (expansion reveals story; the reveal is a scene, not a loot table).

## 13. Visual progression: four stages, one screenshot test

The station reads its own history. Global development stages (per-section, so a growing station wears all of them at once at its frontier):

- **STAGE 1 — REFUGE:** emergency lanterns, rubble, bedrolls, improvised tools, exposed cable runs, leaks, cold concrete.
- **STAGE 2 — FUNCTIONING SHELTER:** stable light in the core, a working galley, organized storage, patched walls, basic sanitation, dedicated work areas.
- **STAGE 3 — SETTLEMENT:** expanded and specialized rooms, real beds and some privacy, community decoration appearing (resident-made), reliable utilities, more people, the first green under grow-lights.
- **STAGE 4 — UNDERGROUND HOME:** permanent architecture, green spaces, public gathering places, art and memorials, the Wire Room's mast lights, distinct neighborhood-like sections with names, and the unmistakable signs that people expect to *remain*.

**The screenshot test (07-tested):** players must recognize progress from a screenshot alone — no resource numbers. Stage transitions are carried by lighting warmth, clutter composition, resident behavior, and decoration density (the existing "light returning" identity, now with a growth axis). The slice delivers Stage 1 → early Stage 2.

## 15. Vertical-slice construction scope ("The First Count", revised)

**Starting condition (Day 1):** one damaged central platform holding **four functional areas** — the **Scrubber Gate** (damaged but working airlock/decon), the **Flywheel Room** (partial power), the **Camp** (a temporary zone: bedrolls + cook ring + improvised bench + **a small transfer pump feeding the emergency water tank** — so a pump link exists in the utility chain on every route), and the improvised **Listening Post** (in the old signal box). Visible around them: dark, blocked, unusable station — **two blocked horizontal paths** (the **East Concourse** behind a rubble collapse: two stranded railcars + kiosk row; the **West Gallery** behind flooding: the cistern main + pump room) and **one inaccessible level** (the **Deep Service Level** behind a jammed freight lift: the Fitters' Shop + parts store). Limited electricity, water (emergency tank), and ventilation.

**The week's exclusive choice (required)** — staged as a community scene, not a menu: labor and materials before the Day 4–5 storm allow opening **either** east (comfort/health/capacity: Sleeper Car, Aid Car, Canteen from the kiosk row) **or** west (water security: Cistern Works + Pump Room, plus a drained-gallery salvage bonus and a storm-recovery labor refund large enough to accelerate the late-week east opening). **Ash advocates east** ("no one turned away" — she wants real triage beds); **Imka advocates west** ("the station outlives us all" — water before comfort). The overruled resident's position is recorded and returns once later in the week as a bark, a Strain tick, or a Graymorn log line when the unchosen wing's absence bites. The unchosen path opens late-week at documented higher cost on either route. The freight lift is a smaller mid-week project (tools from Depot 9 — a single prepared run can carry wire + tools at a real carry-weight tradeoff, itself a commitment decision; V2's cross-check also returns lift schematics that cheapen the project) unlocking the Fitters' Shop.

**West-route drain bootstrap (canon):** the West Gallery is drained with the Camp's transfer pump run off the Flywheel as a temporary rig — slow, charge-hungry, and Fatigue-costly until the proper Pump Room comes online behind it; partial planking (access-only, no water yield, ongoing damp) is the cheap alternative. "Either wing before the storm" is thereby demonstrably true on both routes.

**Route-neutral fallbacks (canon — the west route is a real route, not a trap):**
- *Triage:* without the Aid Car, Ash treats casualties at the Camp/Staging bench at a stated speed/quality penalty with worse Condition outcomes — the room's absence is *felt through a named resident*, not undefined.
- *The Day-5 earned warm beat:* stages in the Canteen on the east route, and as a **cook-ring "canteen corner" variant** (same Maren Accord-anchor mechanics, smaller recovery, different set) on the west route.
- *Berths:* the fifth berth is prepared in the Sleeper Car (east) **or as bunks in a cleared/drained west bay** (west); the capacity beat is satisfiable on both routes by Day 6.
- *The canonical repurpose:* Camp → Staging Room after beds move to the Sleeper Car (east) or to the west-bay bunks (west).
- *The storm cascade (Pillar 1's worked example):* on the west route it runs through the Cistern Works; pre-breakthrough or on the east route, the third link is the **tank-feed stall** (intake clog → scrubber draw → flywheel dip → transfer-pump stall), so the cascade fires as specified on every route.

**Within-wing layout decisions (canon — the slice's free-placement proof, beyond the east/west binary):**
1. **The railcar choice (east):** the player decides which stranded railcar becomes the Sleeper Car and which the Aid Car — one sits nearer the noisy Flywheel bay (sleep quality/Strain), the other nearer the Scrubber Gate (post-run triage distance). This instantiates the **noise** and **travel** tension axes as player decisions.
2. **Cold Store siting:** an east kiosk bay (near the Canteen, on the loaded east node) or a drained west bay (cool, quiet, but a longer haul from the gate) — instantiating **cascade exposure** (node load) vs. travel.
3. **The Level-1 seal placement:** the player chooses which bulkhead receives the slice's seal — protecting the Camp core or the newly opened wing during the storm — instantiating **storm isolation** as a decision with visible consequences on storm night.
4. **The fifth berth's location** (within the chosen wing) — privacy vs. proximity, a small human-ledger placement call.
All four documented tension axes (noise, travel, cascade, isolation) are thereby exercised by *player decisions* in the slice — R-22's evidence base.

**The fifth resident (named, D-039):** the berth candidate is **Juna Malek**, one of the two Foundry Line distress arrivals — a former bicycle courier (runner-capable; the "provisionable as a second runner" payoff of D1), with a one-line history, a value line, a small bark set, a crew-strip seat (+1 on arrival) and a day-report presence if she stays. **If no berth exists, she is not silently dropped:** turning her away is an explicit on-screen gate decision with witnesses — it crosses Ash's "no one turned away" value — and the outcome (stayed / turned away / walked on to the Tidemill) occupies the epilogue's losses-and-people fact slot.

**Expansion reveals story (slice canon — one cheap authored reveal per section, surfaced through the survey report and clear/drain staging, warm-beat-class costed):** the kiosk row's still-shuttered stall with its owner's last inventory chalked on the board; the West Gallery's silt line showing *why* it flooded (the storm drain was blocked deliberately, from inside, during the evacuation); the Deep Service toolroom's abandoned shift log — the fitters who kept the lights on until the end. Opening the station up is how the station tells its past.

**The Breakthrough (warm-beat class):** the moment a wing opens — east rubble falling / west waterline draining, dark bays lighting for the first time — is a staged, register-tagged hope beat with the same presentation weight as the canteen scene (light-return moment + Strain/Accord recovery), in east and west variants. The slice's peak building-fantasy payoff is budgeted content, not incidental.

**Worst case, documented:** a west-route player who built badly (seal on the wrong bulkhead, node overdrawn) and hits the Day 4–5 storm: the free storm telegraph still fires; the crisis has its 3 authored responses; triage runs at the bench penalty; the storm-recovery labor refund plus Deep Service tools (if taken) fund the late-week east opening or the berth bunks; Relay Night arrives with a rougher, poorer, but coherent station. The feasibility sketch (below) must show this path's person-phase math closing with ≥3 of 4 residents functional.

**The Slice Feasibility Sketch (blocking deliverable, D-039):** before the systems stage begins, a first-pass sketch must exist as an appendix to this document: person-phases available per resident per day (net of Fatigue, Teo's run nights, triage, Imka's overwork telegraph), cost ranges per slice verb, construction's material account (which verbs cost vs. yield Salvage — joint with the crafting budget, 07 §9), and a day-by-day critical-path table for **both routes** showing the pre-storm exclusivity window, all §21 beats, and the worst case above. 07 §21's "labor/material math verified" refers to *this artifact*.

**Required slice beats (07 §21 tests these):** survey both blocked paths (+ the lift), each survey returning its story hook; clear ≥1 section (with its authored reveal); build ≥3 functional rooms; upgrade ≥1 room (e.g., Listening Post improvised → wired, or Flywheel partial → stable); complete ≥1 **module expansion** (route-dependent: a pantry module onto the Canteen, or a filtration module onto the Cistern Works); extend ≥1 utility trunk to a new node; repair 1 damaged facility (the Scrubber Gate intake — the storm crisis); repurpose or rearrange ≥1 room (the Camp → Staging Room, on either route's berth path); increase sleeping capacity and **prepare a berth for Juna Malek** (route-appropriate location); make the within-wing placement decisions (railcar roles / Cold Store site / seal bulkhead / berth spot); suffer/handle ≥1 layout-caused consequence (the noise-adjacency Strain effect is mandatory content; the overdrawn-node brownout is additional); have ≥1 construction decision demonstrably touch a named resident's human ledger; see the station change visibly (Stage 1 → early Stage 2, screenshot-testable on both routes); complete ≥1 meaningful Listening Post decision (including ≥1 monitored signal that cheapens or de-risks a construction step — V2's lift schematics or the beacons' pump-state intel) and ≥1 nightrun (active or delegated); reach Relay Night.

**End state:** approximately **8–10 functional areas** (from 4), e.g.: Scrubber Gate (repaired) + Staging · Flywheel (stabilized) · Listening Post (wired) · Sleeper Car · Aid Car *or* Cistern Works + Pump Room · Canteen · Fitters' Shop · a storage room — the exact set depending on the east/west choice. Numbers may be adjusted by feasibility testing (documented reason required), but the slice must prove that **construction, expansion, and shelter growth are central gameplay**.

## 16. Mobile interaction principles (build mode)

- **Player-facing workflows (D-040):** the sixteen verbs remain internal design vocabulary but surface as **five workflows** — RECLAIM · CONNECT · BUILD · OPERATE · ADAPT (mapping in 12 §4). The UI shows only the actions relevant to the selected target; the full vocabulary is never exposed at once. The ten-step reclamation loop (§3) is a design model, not a per-room checklist — different spaces use different subsets (a kiosk: clear→furnish→staff; the flooded gallery: survey→drain→reinforce→connect→build; a railcar: access→repurpose; the damaged gate: repair only).
- **Build mode is a toggled lens** over the same cutaway — not a separate screen. Reclaimable sections ghost in; bays show state (blocked / cleared / shell / furnished).
- **Mode-state rules (D-039):** the build lens **force-enables the engineer's overlay's capacity layer** (a builder always sees node capacity, regardless of the overlay toggle's normal state); the lens announces itself with a visible mode frame/tint and a fixed exit affordance in a named corner; decision cards **queue and never fire** while the lens is open; the monkey test must be unable to issue a construction order from normal mode (07 §17).
- **Zoom granularity:** at overview zoom, build interaction is **section-granularity only** (sections comfortably exceed the 48 dp floor); bay selection and footprint placement are gated to room/section zoom, where bays render tappable. No sub-48 dp target exists at any zoom where it is live (07 §17 audits all build-lens targets at every zoom).
- **Verb cards, two-tier:** tap a section/bay/room → a card of **2–4 contextually-ranked verbs** with quoted physical costs, plus a visible **"all works…" expander** listing every legal verb for that target (ranking rule documented at systems stage). Repurpose and Deconstruct are reachable on any built room in ≤2 taps from its card — the recovery verbs are never buried.
- **Placement grammar:** footprint placement is tap-first — the verb card offers footprint-size chips, the ghost auto-proposes a bay span, tap affordances shift/flip it, confirm commits; **drag-positioning is additionally permitted inside the build lens** (an amendment to 09 A.6's drag rule) with the tap path always available. Ghost previews show clear valid/invalid states. No drag-drawing of pipes or wires, ever (trunk/node model, §8).
- **Tap budgets (two, explicitly):** assignment work orders ≤3 taps from overview (unchanged); construction works orders ≤6 taps **including** lens entry, verb, and confirm — works orders auto-assign the best-fit available resident by default, with reassignment one tap deep.
- **Load-priority board granularity (locks §8 at scale):** the board holds **load-classes, capped at ≤8 rows** (new rooms declare their board class at definition time); individual-room shutoffs live on the room/node panel, never on the board. The board is the **third sanctioned allocation-view exception** (with the radio scheduler and the section survey report) to one-decision-at-a-time.
- **Chrome suppression:** full-screen allocation views (radio scheduler, survey report, load-priority board) suppress the crew strip and resource HUD; "persistent" chrome is persistent in world/overview contexts only (09 A.9).
- Works orders queue through the existing order system; the day report lists construction progress and deferrals.

## 17. Major technical and production risks (registered in 06)

- **R-21 Construction-system scope:** the bay/section/works model is a genuine system spine (data model, UI lens, save state, sim hooks). Mitigations: verbs share the works-order framework; sections are authored data, not procedural geometry; the slice ships a bounded verb subset (survey, clear, drain, reinforce, connect, construct, furnish, staff, upgrade, repair, repurpose, **and one authored expand instance per route** — with excavate/specialize/decorate/deconstruct in reduced or scripted form; deconstruct's slice surface is the repurpose path's stored-equipment mechanics). Descope rung: slice ships the east *or* west arc fully rather than both partially.
- **R-22 Layout meaningfulness:** the risk that one layout dominates (solver play) or none matters (decoration). Mitigations: the four documented tension axes (noise, travel, cascade, isolation) each carry at least one slice-visible consequence; tuning targets "no dominant layout" the way crisis options target "no dominant response" (≤70% convergence tests).
- **Art cost of growth (extends R-19):** four visual stages × sections multiplies the modular kit. Mitigations: stages share geometry with lighting/clutter/decal deltas; decoration is an instanced prop vocabulary; the keystone scene gains a per-stage variant.
- **Save/state growth (extends R-10):** bay states, works progress, and layout history join the world state; the save-kill-restore rig covers mid-works saves from first playable.
- **Population ceiling (extends R-04/R-05):** 12–20 named residents is a *test target*; the named-cast identity survives at whatever ceiling the perf/animation/authoring tests support, and the number is revisited at the systems stage with evidence.
- **Slice cost (extends R-14):** the building revision raises the full-table slice estimate; see the re-costed R-14 (≈10–14 solo months full-table; ≈6–8 descoped). The descope ladder now protects the building core: active nightruns descope before construction does, and the slice's construction proof is non-negotiable (completion gate).
